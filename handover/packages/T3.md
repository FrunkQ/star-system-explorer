# T3: NAV, astrogation (19 Nov 2026)

Design handoff 6.8. Build plan row: "route candidates, hazards, difficulty, drift states"; checks "fixture transfers
match analytic solutions; 'ship not under power' returns ways out". Needs P1. Physics track.

## What was built
- **New crate** `crates/sse-nav` (`nav-v1`), with settings under `provides.other.nav` in the new pack
  `packs/base-nav`. Every number in the hazard and difficulty rules is pack data: hanging config for Orac to tune.
  `NavSettings::check` refuses unknown difficulty reasons, reasons without points, and a rescue ship that is not a
  template.
- **Orbit maths** (`kepler.rs`):
  - elements to state;
  - universal-variable propagation for any conic, with a fixed number of Newton steps;
  - single-revolution prograde Lambert by bisection on z, 200 steps (Curtis 2014, algorithms 3.4 and 5.2).
  - Stumpff C uses 2 sin²(√z/2)/z, so it stays exact next to Lambert's 4π² bound, where 1 − cos cancels to 0.
- **`plan(settings, system, request)`** returns a `RoutePlan`.
  - **Request**:
    - a ship (template or object);
    - `from` and `to`, each a body (address or name) or a state `{r_m, v_m_s}` around the star;
    - a departure time and window;
    - `prefer`: `fastest`, `save-fuel` or `balanced`.
  - **Candidates**:
    - **Hohmann**, the textbook transfer between two bodies, at the best phase in the window. It is labelled
      ideal (circular, coplanar).
    - **Lambert arcs** on a grid of departure times by flight time (0.4 to 1.6 × the Hohmann time). The cheapest
      grid point is refined by six rounds of a 5 × 5 search that halves its steps. Three picks:
      - save fuel;
      - fastest within the fuel;
      - balanced (Δv × time).
    - **Torch flights** for sustained drives. Each flies a straight line with a turnover, and a fixed point (20
      steps) follows the moving target. It burns all the way when the fuel allows, otherwise accelerates, coasts
      and brakes at half the Δv given. Three tries: 100%, 50% and 20% of the usable Δv.
    - **End burns**: escape and capture between a parking orbit (1.1 radii) and the hyperbolic excess, using the
      Oberth form √(v∞² + 2μ/r) − √(μ/r).
  - **Each candidate carries**:
    - burns, days, Δv, propellant by Tsiolkovsky and the reserve left, and peak g;
    - radiation dose and the chance of a large solar particle event;
    - peak stellar flux and closest approach to the star;
    - one-way light delays (home at arrival, and the longest on the way);
    - hazards;
    - difficulty (score, label and reasons);
    - `why` lines, and a 64-point track for drawing.
  - **Ordering**: candidates over the usable Δv go to `refused`, with what they need. The rest are sorted best first
    by the preference, and `recommended` is the first.
- **Hazards** (`hazards.rs`):
  - **Cosmic rays**: 1.84 mSv/day (MSL RAD in cruise, Zeitlin et al. 2013), scaled by extra shielding.
  - **Solar storms**: by time spent close to the star, scaled as (r / 1 au)⁻² × luminosity.
  - **Belts**: giants' radiation belts, both on close passes and when a giant is the start or end of the trip.
  - **Grazes**: passes within 3 radii of a body that is not an end.
  - **The star**: closer than 10 stellar radii.
  - **Debris**: days spent inside a belt (Main Belt, Kuiper Belt).
  - **Heat**: stellar flux over the ship's limit.
  - **Gravity** on torch flights: flagged as approximate when the star's pull exceeds 10% of the thrust.
  - **Long burns**: burns over 10% of the trip, for finite-burn losses.
  - **Difficulty**: points per reason from the pack. Bands: routine, easy, moderate, hard, very hard. The trip dose
    limit is 50 mSv (ICRP 103's limit for one year).
- **Drift** (`drift.rs`), a ship not under power: `to` is absent and `from` is a state.
  - **Orbit**: bound or not, e, a, periapsis and apoapsis, period, and whether it is heading inwards.
  - **Fate**:
    - falls into the star, with the time from Kepler's equation (elliptic or hyperbolic);
    - leaves the system;
    - stays in orbit, or is too hot at periapsis.
  - **Encounters**: closest passes within 3 Hill radii, soonest first.
  - **Ways out**, feasible first, then soonest:
    - keep drifting;
    - burn into a circular orbit at the present distance;
    - burn for each body (the cheapest course from the drifting state);
    - coast to an encounter (capture there, or use its pull);
    - call for rescue from each port: the light delay, then the rescue ship's fastest course to the moving wreck,
      marked too late if the drift ends first.
- **Stock ships** in the pack:
  - chemical transfer ship (4.4 km/s exhaust, mass ratio 5);
  - nuclear thermal ship (8.8 km/s);
  - fusion torch (1 g, fictional);
  - rescue cutter (2 g, fictional).
  The Traveller pack will add its own.
- **Kernel op `plan_route`** (`crates/sse-kernel/src/nav.rs`). The system comes from one of:
  - `fixture: "sol"` (JPL approximate J2000 elements);
  - an inline `nav` system;
  - an adopted `system` in the open universe at `at_s`. Planets come from its SystemSpec; radii from the surface
    bodies (or a rocky mass-radius rule); luminosity from `star.luminosity`; radius from L and T_eff; belts from
    the debris disc; `ports` names the bodies with ports.

  The response is `route_planned { plan }`. Errors: `unknown_ship` (lists the templates), `unknown_fixture`,
  `route_refused` (an unknown body, the same body twice, ways out from a body), `bad_request`, `no_universe`,
  `no_orbits`. Medium cost.

## Checks (`cargo test -p sse-nav`, `cargo test -p sse-kernel plan_route`)
- **Lambert**: arcs on short-way, long-way, hyperbolic and inclined cases reach their targets within 10⁻⁶ when
  propagated. Opposite points are refused as degenerate.
- **Propagation**: agrees with Kepler's elements over 5000 days within 10⁻⁷. A hyperbola keeps its energy.
- **Analytic fixtures** (circular coplanar Earth and Mars):
  - Hohmann takes 258.9 days with burns of 2.94 and 2.65 km/s, the textbook values;
  - the cheapest Lambert arc matches Hohmann's Δv within 3% and its time within 10%.
- **Torch** (a star too light to matter, 1 au apart):
  - t = 2√(d/a) and Δv = a t exactly;
  - with less fuel, t = d/v + v/a, and the burns add up to the trip.
- **Free fall** from rest at 1 au: falls into the Sun in the analytic free-fall time (64.5 days) within 10⁻³.
  Circularising needs the 29.8 km/s circular speed, which a chemical ship lacks. Earth and Mars rescues are offered,
  and at least one is in time.
- **A drifting ship near Earth** stays in orbit, meets Earth, and can burn for it.
- **Sol**:
  - Earth to Mars by chemical ship: coasting only, 100 to 400 days, a dose over the limit, delays of minutes;
  - a torch to Jupiter: under 15 days, its belts and the Main Belt flagged;
  - towards Mercury the flux exceeds 5 kW/m²;
  - a weak ship to Neptune is refused with what it needs.
- **Determinism**: the same question gives the same plan.
- **Golden**: `tests/golden/nav.json` (new) covers Earth to Mars, chemical and torch, and the drift at 1 au.
- **Contract**: six new cases. `contract.jsonl` was re-blessed on purpose: the `hello` op list and the unknown-op
  message gain `plan_route`, plus the new lines. TS was regenerated.

## Known limits and next steps
- **Model**:
  - Patched conics only: planets pull at the ends, not along the way. There are no gravity-assist courses yet,
    though encounters are listed for drift.
  - Moons are not yet bodies NAV flies between.
  - Single-revolution Lambert only.
- **"Stay hidden behind the planet"** (design 6.8) is not built yet. It needs line-of-sight from observers
  (track O sensing) and becomes a constraint plus a hazard.
- **Not yet chronicle events**: courses do not become events once the GM approves; that is V5 intents and GM
  approvals. Drift reads no ship state from the universe yet: constructs arrive with X3d.
- **Radiation is rough**: the attenuation length, solar particle event rates and belt dose rates are rules for play,
  marked rough in the pack's sources.
- **Torch courses**: the straight line ignores the star's pull when it is small (flagged when it is not), and the
  velocity matching at the end is added rather than blended into the flight.
- **References to verify**:
  - Curtis, Vallado and Zeitlin et al. were written from memory and need their DOIs checked, like the earlier
    packages.
  - The Sol elements in `tests/fixtures/nav/sol.json` are JPL's approximate J2000 elements, also written from
    memory.
