# U22: A world through time, the controls (UI track)

Status: done 22 Nov 2026. This is the chart room's half of the globe time lapse for Alex's system-generation video. The renderer track did the layer passes, the frames player and the globe API (R6 parts 1 and 2, PRs 123 and 140). Proven core did `surface_frames` (PR 135). This package replaces the interim control on the Body screen with `packages/views/body/src/BodyTimeline.svelte`.

## What it does

- **Through time** loads the world's history, through Body.svelte's `lapse` (the kernel's `surface_frames`). "Back to today" closes it.
- **Layer toggles.** One layer at a time, as a radio group named "Layer": Land and sea, Plates, Volcanism, Surface temperature, Rain and snow, Ice and glaciers, Water running off. A layer whose fields the kernel lists as pending is disabled and says why. The layer's legend sits under the scrubber, with its stated limits under that.
- **Scrubber.** A "When" range over the world's own clock (Myr from formation). It is headed "3.97 billion years ago · Earth at 600 million years".
- **Event marks** above the scrubber. The world's dated events (Earth: 447) are binned into 90 marks so hundreds stay readable. Each mark's height says how many events fall in it. Impacts and flood volcanism are in accent ink; plate events (collisions, splits, sutures, pole jumps) are in quiet ink. A thin accent line shows the time in view.
- **Play** steps through the keyframes about two a second from where the scrubber is, and pauses on a second press. Moving the scrubber or choosing an event stops it.
- **"Why is this here?" card.** Clicking a mark moves time to its most telling event: one with a place first, then an impact or flood, else the first. The card shows:
  - the event in the kernel's words;
  - when it happened, and where (latitude and longitude), or "across the world";
  - at Curious depth, the height, temperature and ice under it then;
  - at Science depth, every field and the sea level of the time;
  - at Engine depth, the cell number as well;
  - the kernel's own why lines.

  When the scrubber rests near an event, a quiet "<event> · why?" link offers the card.
- If the globe runtime gains `lapse.view(lon, lat)`, the globe turns to the event (asked of the renderer thread). BodyTimeline already calls it when it exists.
- Nothing here computes physics, and the state hash never moves.

## Checks

- `tests/ui/render.e2e.mjs` (R6 block, updated here) checks:
  - layers are chosen by the "Layer" radio group;
  - the events are marked (more than 10);
  - clicking an impact or flood mark opens the why card with the event in words;
  - Play moves time forward;
  - Back to today closes the lapse;
  - the state hash does not move.
- The renderer's own checks on frames, layers and pick are in `packages/renderer/test/timelapse.test.ts`.
