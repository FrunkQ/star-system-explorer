# B1: one body, physics by condition (started 19 Nov 2026)

Plan and rulings: `handover/design/SSE4-star-census.md` section 3. Alex, 19 Nov: stars and planets are one kind of
data object, and the work should come "early, or at least accommodate until made". The kernel thread takes the atlas
half (derived `body.kind`). The UI thread takes the views (luminous, has-a-surface and root predicates). The light
thread (`sse-light`, track O) turns B1's condition facts into spectra.

## B1a: one light rule on one mass axis (this step)
- **`sse_formation::body::self_light(fits, mass, age, radius?)`** answers, for any body, whether it shines by itself
  and why, from its mass and age only:
  - from the deuterium limit (0.0124 Msun, about 13 Jupiter masses) to 100 Msun: the life line (fusion,
    contraction before the main sequence, cooling remnants);
  - brown dwarfs: deuterium burning while young (about 10 Myr at the limit, growing as mass squared), then
    cooling;
  - from about 10 Earth masses to the deuterium limit: a giant planet cooling on the same Burrows et al. 2001 law
    as brown dwarfs, so the two meet with no seam at 13 Jupiter masses (tested to 2%);
  - below that: no light of its own (its heat leaves as heat flow, which P1's heat budget carries).
  It returns luminosity, effective temperature, radius, the source (`fusion`, `deuterium`, `contraction`,
  `cooling`, `none`), the phase and a `why`.
- **Facts**: adopted formation systems now publish `body.light_source` on every star, and `star.luminosity`,
  `star.effective_temperature` and `body.light_source` on every giant planet, under the same field names stars
  use, so anything that reads light from a body reads it from any body.

## Checks

  of its own; Jupiter glows with 1e-10 to 1e-9 L_sun at 60 to 110 K; a 20 Jupiter-mass brown dwarf at 1 Gyr is a
  T dwarf (500 to 1300 K) and burns deuterium at 5 Myr; no seam at the deuterium limit.
- `contract.jsonl` re-blessed on purpose: the adopted system's facts gain `body.light_source` (and the events'

## B1c part 1: stability tag for multiples
- `sse_formation::stars::critical_ratio` (Mardling and Aarseth 2001 eq. 90) and `stability(stars)`: every outer
  orbit of a hierarchical multiple is tested against the orbit inside it and tagged stable or unstable with a
  `why`; nothing is refused, so authored configurations stand, tagged.
- Adopted systems publish `orbit.stability` and `orbit.stability_why` on each outer companion.
- Check `mardling_aarseth_tags_unstable_triples`: the equal-mass circular ratio, an Alpha Centauri-like triple
  stable, an authored 2:1 triple tagged unstable. `contract.jsonl` re-blessed on purpose (the Kestrel triple's
  third star gains the two facts; hashes after it move).
- Still to do (B1c part 2): pairs of pairs (a close pair as one barycentre object inside a wider pair, nested to any
  depth). Today's structure is a chain, each companion around everything inside it.

## B1c part 2: pairs of pairs (held locally behind PR 41)
- `sse_formation::hierarchy`: a multiple is a tree. `Node::Body {id, mass_msun}` or `Node::Pair {a, b, orbit}` with
  `PairOrbit {semi_major_axis_au, eccentricity, inclination_deg}`, nested to any depth, serialised as tagged JSON so an
  authored system is plain data.
- `check(root)` tests every pair whose side is itself a pair with Mardling and Aarseth, the inner pair against the
  other side as perturber, worst margin reported, mutual inclination honoured. Unstable is tagged with a `why`, never
  refused. Each pair also gets its total mass, Kepler period and a path ("", "a", "ab", ...).
- `from_chain(stars)` turns the generator's chain into a tree; the root check equals `stars::stability` exactly.
- Checks `cargo test -p sse-formation hierarchy`: chain and tree agree; a 2+2 quadruple 100 au apart stable and 4 au
  apart unstable; a Castor-like sextuple (three close pairs, depth 3) stable at every level; inclination lowers the
  critical ratio by the 0.3 i/pi term. No golden changed (nothing publishes the tree yet).
- Next: the generator draws 2+2 and deeper trees (Tokovinin 2014 statistics), adopted systems publish the tree, and
  the authoring path (hand-built systems) reads `Node` JSON.

## B1d part 1: one dynamo law as editable blocks
- `sse-atoms/src/magnetic.rs`, three atoms, and the flow `packs/base-physics/flows/body-magnetism.json`
  (hook `body.magnetism`):
  - `sse.magnetic.dynamo_density`: core mass over core volume for a rocky world (a giant's metallic-hydrogen region
    can be given directly);
  - `sse.magnetic.dynamo_field`: Christensen, Holzwarth and Reiners 2009 energy-flux scaling,
    B²/2μ₀ = c f_ohm ρ^(1/3) q^(2/3) (Christensen 2010: c 0.63, f_ohm 0.9). One law for planets and rapidly rotating
    stars, so it fits the one-body plan; no convected heat means no dynamo;
  - `sse.magnetic.surface_dipole`: the dipole share at the dynamo surface, falling as r⁻³, and the moment
    μ = 4π B_eq R³/μ₀. Publishes `body.surface_magnetic_field` and `body.magnetic_moment` (the fact the light
    thread reads).
- Core gains the dimensions `MAGNETIC_FIELD` and `MAGNETIC_MOMENT` and the units `t`, `ut`, `gauss`, `a_m2`.
- Checks (flow golden cases): Earth with 10 TW out of the core (Lay et al. 2008) lands within 20% of IGRF's
  7.7e22 A m² and 30 µT; Jupiter with Juno's internal flux (Li et al. 2018) through a dynamo at 0.85 R_J lands
  within 25% of JRM09's 1.56e27 A m² and 4.1 G; no flux gives no field. Atom count 37 to 40 (P1.md).
- Goldens on purpose: `packs/base-physics/pack.json` and its fixture copy (new atoms, kinds, units and flow);
  `contract.jsonl` (the stack hash and flow list in `stack_loaded`, and ids after it).
- Fudges F-S29 (dipole share tuned to Earth and Jupiter) and F-S30 (total, not superadiabatic, flux).
- Reference details written from memory for the citations thread to verify: Christensen 2010 eq. 12 constants, Li et
  al. 2018 (Nature Communications 9, 3709) Jupiter flux, Lay et al. 2008 CMB heat flow.
