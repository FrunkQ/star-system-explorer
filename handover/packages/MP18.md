# MP18: water's ices, the data wave

Track MP (master data sheet), design `handover/design/SSE4-matter-pack-design.md` section 4.3A. Alex, 22 Nov 00:50:
"we need a LOT more ice types all overlaid on Water ... the ice that created Neptune's mag field should be
calculable, and worked out from that table". This package is the data. MP17 (kernel thread) reads it and
answers `phase_at`; MP19 walks interiors through it; MP20 finds dynamo layers from its conductivities.

## What was built

- `scripts/water_phases.py`: every phase of water, the curves between them, the triple points, and the
  lookup table built from those same curves (so the table and the boundaries cannot disagree). Python
  standard library only; deterministic. `python3 scripts/water_phases.py` prints an ASCII map of the
  phase diagram for a quick look.
- `scripts/build-base-reference.py` puts it under `substances[h2o].phases`, beside the existing
  triple_point, vapour_pressure and density fields (unchanged, so W8 and every other reader of the three
  familiar phases is untouched). Layout agreed with the kernel thread:
  - `phases.forms`: 16 stable phases and 12 metastable ones, each with names in the three wordings, kind,
    stability, evidence (grade, the T and p it holds for, source) and props (density for all; compression
    for III, V and VI; electrical conductivity where a source gives one).
  - `phases.boundaries`: 36 curves, each `between` two stable phases, with its form and range, its
    evidence grade and source. Forms are listed at the top of `water_phases.py`.
  - `phases.meets`: 21 triple points, each written once. The ice Ih, liquid and vapour point equals
    `phases.triple_point_k` and `triple_point_pa`, and the build refuses the pack if they differ.
  - `phases.table`: 4401 pressures (1e-10 Pa to 1 TPa, 0.005 dex) by 1501 temperatures (10 K to 10,000 K,
    0.002 dex), log on both axes. It is stored run-length encoded (`rle`): rows run low pressure first and
    are split by "/"; runs run low temperature first and are split by spaces; each run is the phase's
    base-36 digit followed by its length in base 36. That is 59 KB for 6.6 million cells. The grid had to
    be this fine for the kernel's boundary check to pass. Ice Ih and III sit only 4 MPa apart, and near the
    ice VII, X and superionic point the regions are a few hundredths of a decade wide. At 0.05 dex, 12 of
    the 36 boundaries failed.
  - Curves are written in the two forms the kernel checks. `iapws_melt` (π = 1 + Σ a(1 − θ^b), or ln π = Σ
    a(1 − θ^b) when `log`) covers the IAPWS melting curves of Ih, III, V, VI and VII, and Frank et al.'s
    Simon fit rewritten in that form. `points` ([[p_pa, t_k], ...], straight in T and log p) covers
    everything else, sampled from its equation, with the equation kept under `fit`. The table is built from
    the same straight-in-log-p lines, so the two agree by construction.
- Build-time checks (`validate_phases`), the same ones MP17 runs at load:
  - every boundary and triple point names stable forms;
  - each triple point's three pairs have boundaries;
  - the table has n_p × n_t cells;
  - every stable phase appears in the table;
  - two touching cells are either the same phase, joined by a boundary, or separated by one phase thinner
    than a cell (this happens only near the ice VII, X and superionic point);
  - evidence grades are measured, computed, extrapolated or convention;
  - a computed phase or boundary names a source with a DOI or URL (Alex, 22 Nov, ruling 23: "computed and
    link to source").
- A local copy of the kernel's along-boundary check (cross each boundary by 1.5 grid steps in T and in p;
  at least 80% of samples must show both phases) passes on all 35 boundaries that reach inside the table.
  Ice XI against vapour lies wholly below 1e-10 Pa, and the kernel skips it.
- CI already fails a stale sheet (PR 111), so the table is covered by `build-base-reference.py --check`.

## The stable phases

| Phase | id | Where it is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| vapour, liquid | h2o.vapour, h2o.liquid | as usual | measured (IAPWS) |
| supercritical fluid | h2o.supercritical | above 647.096 K and 22.064 MPa | convention: no phase change, the name changes |
| ionic fluid | h2o.ionic_fluid | above 2000 K and 20 GPa | computed; the edge is recalled, unverified |
| ice Ih, ice XI | h2o.ice_ih, h2o.ice_xi | ordinary ice; its ordered form below 72 K | measured |
| ice II, III, V, VI | h2o.ice_ii and so on | icy moons' deep ice | measured |
| ice XV | h2o.ice_xv | ordered ice VI below about 130 K | measured, edge approximate |
| ice VII, VIII | h2o.ice_vii, h2o.ice_viii | 2.1 GPa up; VIII the cold ordered form | measured |
| ice X | h2o.ice_x | above about 60 GPa | measured cold, computed hot |
| superionic ice, bcc (XX) | h2o.ice_xx | 35 to 75 GPa above the superionic onset | computed |
| superionic ice, fcc (XVIII) | h2o.ice_xviii | above 75 GPa and about 2000 K | measured (shock) and computed |

The metastable rows are Ic, IV, IX, XII, XIII, XIV, XVI, XVII and XIX, plus the low-, high- and
very-high-density amorphous ices. They hold their density and how each is made, and stay off the map (ruling 22).

## Where the curves come from

- **IAPWS R14-08(2011)**: melting of Ih, III, V, VI and VII, and sublimation. All six of the release's
  check values reproduce to the printed digits, which also confirmed two signs and exponents that the PDF
  text had dropped.
- **IAPWS SR1-86(1992)**: boiling. Both check values reproduce. That fixed a5 = −15.9618719, whose minus
  sign the PDF text had dropped.
- **Frank, Fei and Hu 2004**: ice VII melting above 585.3 K. IAPWS's ice VII curve holds to 715 K in
  name, but its 22nd-power term runs away above about 650 K. Frank et al.'s Simon fit (3 to 60 GPa) crosses
  it at 585.3 K, where the two agree to 0.1%, so the curve hands over there with no step. Measurements
  disagree by about 80 K at 13 GPa (Lin et al. 2004).
- **Solid-solid triple points**: from the LSBU phase-diagram table, which cites a primary paper for each,
  checked against Wikipedia's table. Where the two differ, the note says so. II-V-VI is the main case: 218 K
  and 620 MPa, or 201.9 K and 670.8 MPa. The lines between triple points are straight. The low-temperature
  ends (II-XI, II-XV, XV-VIII, VIII-X) are those lines carried on, and marked extrapolated.
- **Ice VII to X**: Lin et al. 2005 (60 GPa at 300 K, quoting Goncharov et al. 1999, with a negative slope).
- **Superionic ice**:
  - Cheng et al. 2021: bcc below about 75 GPa, onset near 2000 K, melting at 3250 K at 100 GPa.
  - Millot et al. 2019: fcc ice XVIII at 100 to 400 GPa and 2000 to 3000 K.
  - Lin et al. 2005: the break in the melting curve at 35 GPa.
  - These give the shape. The lines between the points are straight in T and log p, and graded computed.

## Contested, and marked so

- The point where ice VII, superionic ice and fluid meet is placed anywhere from 14.6 GPa and 850 K
  (Queyroux et al. 2020, quoted by Cheng et al.) to about 47 GPa and 1000 K. Here it sits at Lin et al.'s
  35 GPa, on Frank et al.'s melting curve: 852.3 K.
- The melting of superionic ice beyond 100 GPa. The 5000 K at 190 GPa point is recalled from Millot et al.
  2018 (not read), and the curve beyond is extrapolated.
- The ionic-fluid edge, and the conductivities of ionic and liquid water, are recalled (Redmer et al. 2011;
  Mattsson and Desjarlais 2006), not read. They carry `check: "unverified"` and their sources are
  registered as unverified.
- Densities come from the LSBU structural table, which gives one figure per phase at its own conditions.
  Ice VIII's is 1460 there and 1490 on LSBU's other page. Only III, V and VI have compression data
  (Journaux 2020 Table 3). The other dense ices need an equation of state before MP19 can use them at
  depth.

## Checks run

- IAPWS check values: melting at 260, 254, 265, 320 and 550 K; sublimation at 230 K; saturation at 273.16
  and 373.1243 K. All reproduce.
- 31 test points across the map (1 atm water, steam and ice; Ganymede-depth ices; ice VII at 3 GPa; the
  superionic and ionic regions). The three that first disagreed were wrong expectations near a boundary,
  checked by hand.
- Every triple point has its three phases within 3% of it, except two where the boundaries meet at a sharp
  angle: (VII, XX, supercritical) and (VII, liquid, supercritical). Both are confirmed by point tests just
  inside.
- `scripts/check.sh` passes, and no golden changed.

Goldens: none changed.
