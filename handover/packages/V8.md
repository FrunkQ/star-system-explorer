## What was built
- **`ui-contract/src/route.ts`** (pure parts, no physics):
  - `navFromScene(scene)`: the system NAV reads, from any chart scene. The primary star's mass, and its luminosity
    and radius as the base-physics flows derive them; each planet orbiting the star directly, with `a`, `e` and the
    scene's phase as the mean longitude at the scene's "now". It says what it leaves out (moons, bodies without a
    mass, inclinations) instead of hiding it. Belts come from the scene's belt bands.
  - `shortlist` (one course of each kind first, so three torch throttles don't crowd out a coasting arc),
    `safestFirst` ("Keep us safe": difficulty, then dose), `fuelLeft` against the ship's reserve, words for days,
    Δv, dose, light delay and "in N days" on the campaign clock.
  - Chart geometry in the star's frame (rendering rules 1 to 3): `routeFrame` sizes a linear frame to the course
    and both ends' orbits in f64; `trackPath` and `trackPoint` turn the kernel's 64-point track into pixel numbers;
    `orbitEllipse` draws an orbit from its elements (geometry only); `trackIndexAt` places burns and hazards, which
    sse-nav times from departure.
- **`ViewSession.planRoute`** (kernel `plan_route`). Any seat may plan against an inline system (what it knows);
  planning against an adopted `system` reads the full record, so it is GM only.
- **View `sse.view.route`** (`packages/views/route`), mounted on a new GM screen **Route** (since U6, a section of the Explore tab):
  - left, "Tell the computer": ship (base-nav stock ships), from, to, how long the crew can wait, what matters most
    (fastest, save fuel, balance, keep us safe), what the ship has (Δv aboard, usable after the reserve, top g),
    and the honest notes from `navFromScene`;
  - the chosen course drawn in the star's frame (orbits, belts, the track, burns, hazards, "Tarn at departure",
    "Vault at arrival"), the strip (depart and arrive from now, fuel after arrival against the reserve, Δv, peak g,
    dose, light delay home, closest to the star), burns, why it is hard, how the computer worked it out, what is
    beyond this ship, and the model's own limits;
  - up to three course cards beside it (mini chart, days, fuel left, peak g, difficulty pips, dose, hazards).
  - Defaults: from the first world in the temperate zone, to the next world out. A planet picked on the chart
    becomes the destination. Departure is the campaign's "now" when the workspace carries `campaign_now_yr`
    (U11), else the chart's.
  - Theme tokens only; no theme code. Params for Orac: ship, prefer, window_days, shown.
- **Registration hook** agreed with the chart room thread: `packages/shell/src/screens.more.ts` (`MORE_VIEWS`,
  `MORE_SCREENS`) and `screens.more.css` (layouts keyed on `data-screen`). The shell's only edits are three spread
  lines in `registry.ts` and `App.svelte`. Later screens from this track register there.

## NAV radiation belts: the MF0 stop-gap (22 Nov 2026)
Alex accepted the magnetic fields design (track MF, 22 Nov 01:06, "agree to all", question 10 included). NAV gave
radiation belts only to bodies over 200 Earth masses, so Saturn and Earth had none and every giant had the same dose.
- `NavBody.magnetic_moment_a_m2` (optional). The Sol fixture carries measured moments (Mercury, Earth, Jupiter,
  Saturn, Uranus, Neptune; sources in its `about`). Venus and Mars have none.
- `hazards::belt_reach_radii`: the magnetopause standoff from pressure balance against the solar wind (1.6 nPa at
  1 au, falling as the distance squared): Earth about 10 radii; Mercury about 1.5, so no belts. Belts reach the
  standoff or 15 radii, whichever is nearer. With no moment known (resolved systems today), bodies of at least
  10 Earth masses take belts (all giants have dynamos).
- `hazards::belt_strength`: the 50 mSv an hour rate scaled by the surface field over Jupiter's.
- A course that starts or ends at a body with belts crosses them once at escape speed (was: a flat day). A belt
  dose under 10 mSv (leaving Earth: about 5) is listed but adds no difficulty.
- **Golden `tests/golden/nav.json` changed on purpose**: Earth to Mars plans now list Earth's belts (two plan
  digests); no difficulty label changed. `tests/golden/contract.jsonl`: the two `plan_route`
  cases from Earth now carry Earth's belts.
Next (MF7, when the light and nebulae thread lands it): NAV reads the cut-off, dose and belts from MF7 instead.

## Part 3c: fuel by ship, engine and fuel type (22 Nov 2026)
Alex (22 Nov 00:40): "fuel is determined by ship and engine and fuel type - all physically modelled. FLT is abstract by
nature - and need per system rules ... Real travel & relativistic are always available IF it can break the
gravitational field. Refuelling is a ship capability - it may need a port and exotic fuel." Design section 8,
questions 1 and 2.
- **`sse-nav/src/refuel.rs`**: refuelling kinds as pack data (`nav.voyage.refuel.kinds`: `skim` needs a scoop and
  gives hydrogen, deuterium, helium-3; `ice` needs an ice processor and gives water, hydrogen, hydrogen-oxygen,
  deuterium; `port` needs docking and gives the common fuels). A place lists kind ids; a port adds stock as
  `port:antimatter,tritium` (`NavBody.stock` in a system). `RefuelRules::usable` says why not in words ("needs a
  scoop", "has no helium-3").
- **Ships** gain `fuels` (what the engines burn) and `refuel` (capabilities; none listed means any, so custom ships
  keep working); a jump drive gains `fuel` (default hydrogen) and `limit_diameters` (a setting's jump limit,
  checked against each star). Stock ships: chemical burns hydrogen-oxygen, nuclear thermal hydrogen, the fusion
  torch deuterium and helium-3 (so it refuels at giants, or ports that stock helium-3), the rescue cutter deuterium,
  the jump courier hydrogen (scoop, ice and docking), the antimatter probe antimatter (only ports that stock it).
- **The finder** refuels only where the place, the ship and its fuels agree; a gamble on an unsurveyed system needs
  a ship that can skim (the odds are of a giant). A refusal names the mismatch ("none of the fuel ... suits it").
- **Climbing out**: a flight between stars pays (√2 − 1)√(GM/r) from the temperate orbit (√L au, at least
  10 stellar radii) at each end by the rocket equation, before the cruise; `NoFlight::Well` refuses in words. A jump
  limit adds the flight out to it and in from it at the ship's thrust (2√(d/a)), 0 when the temperate orbit is
  already beyond it (the Sun's 100 diameters is 0.93 au).
- **Kernel `plan_voyage`** gives each system its star's well (mass, luminosity, radius from L and Teff), uses
  positions at the asked time (0.1 Myr frames since PR 116), and adds `fuels` and `refuel` to the reply.
- Goldens change on purpose: `tests/golden/nav.json` (three plan digests; ships now carry `fuels` and `refuel`), and
  `contract.jsonl` (the `plan_route` replies for the same reason; the `plan_voyage` replies gain `fuels`, `refuel`,
  the wells and positions at the asked time).

Honest limits: the jump limit's flight out costs time only (no propellant: settings with jump limits use their own
manoeuvre drives); the star's well is the primary's alone; port stock comes only from `NavBody.stock` or a
`port:` entry (no economy yet, 4.1).

## NAV belts from the field model (MF7 part 2, 22 Nov 2026)
The light and nebulae thread's `read_field` now computes electron belts with dose behind 0.3 to 40 g/cm² of
aluminium, bremsstrahlung included (PR 164, PR 177). NAV reads them:
- `NavBody.belts: Option<BeltProfile>` (shells of distance against dose per shield). `BeltProfile::rate_sv_h`
  interpolates log-linearly in distance and log-log in shielding, zero outside the belts, and never extrapolates
  past the thickest shield the model gives.
- Hazards: a course integrates the rate along its sampled path behind the ship's `shield_g_cm2`; a course that
  starts or ends at the body adds one radial crossing at escape speed. Bodies without a profile keep the MF0
  stand-in (Chapman-Ferraro reach, dose scaled by surface field).
- Kernel `nav::fill_belts` (for `plan_route` and `plan_voyage`): Sol's bodies take the `solar-system` field reads
  (measured dipoles, the wind at each planet, moons and Io's plasma); other bodies with a moment are read with the
  pack's wind (`wind_pressure_1au_pa`, new `wind_speed_m_s` 400 km/s), falling as the distance squared. It needs the
  data sheet's electron and proton constants, so a kernel with no `base-reference` loaded keeps the stand-in
  (the contract fixtures run that way, so goldens did not change).
- Results (Sol, stock ships): leaving Earth behind 20 g/cm² about 3 mSv; arriving at Jupiter by torch behind
  40 g/cm² about 430 mSv over a day.
Limits (the field thread's F40 to F42): electron belts only, filled after strong injections (often ten times
quiet
levels); no proton belt yet, so Earth's thick-shield dose reads low; the belt is a centred dipole in the equator
plane and NAV uses distance only, not magnetic latitude.

## Space highways, part 1: links in the voyage finder (23 Nov 2026)
Alex (23 Nov 19:21 to 19:25): SSE3's "space highways", links between stars such as a stargate route, belong in SSE4
to build on. A link's ends follow the FTL start and end conditions as options and may sit on, over or inside a world.
Two-way by default with a one-way option; a link with no rule is a charted lane flown with the ship's own drive;
links can be secret, through the one visibility control the app will share (a separate design).
- `sse-nav/src/links.rs`: `Link {id, name, kind (a label), a, b (LinkEnd {at: address, place: FTL places}), one_way,
  rule: Option<FtlRule>, counted (MP16 measure id to value)}` and `Links {links, only}`.
  - A link with a rule makes its own leg (`means: "link"`), timed by the rule's duration from the real distance and
    the ship's drive rating (1 without one), fictional when the rule is, needing no jump fuel.
  - A charted lane marks the ship's own jumps and flights along it (`Leg.link`), and `only` keeps every leg to a link.
  - `system_of` places an end inside a system by its address prefix, so a gate on a surface site belongs to its system.
  - `counted_words`: "2 jumps · 7.3 pc measured".
- `voyage::plan_with_links` (and `plan` calls it with none). `Leg.link` is skipped when empty, so goldens are unchanged.
- Tests (`links_tests.rs`): a gate reaches a star 10 pc off that Jump-2 cannot, in its fixed day, both ways; a one-way
  gate refuses the way back; lanes-only takes two lane jumps where a free ship jumps straight; a lanes-only setting
  with no link says why; an end on a surface site belongs to its system.
- Not yet: the climb from each end's place to where the leg may start (the end's FTL places are carried, not flown);
