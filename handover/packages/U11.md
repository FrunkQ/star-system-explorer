# U11: Human-scale time on the ruler (UI track)

Status: parts 1 and 2 done 19 Nov 2026. Asked by Alex the same day: the Gyr ruler is good for initial editing, but play needs human time sliders "like before" (V3), and "do NOT slavishly copy every V3 feature. If there is a better way of doing it, do it."

## What it does

- **One ruler, every scale.** The time ruler now shows a window that zooms (mouse wheel about the pointer, the − and + buttons, the + and − keys, or the Show list: whole history, 10 million years, 1,000 years, 10 years, a year, a month, a week, a day). Its register follows the window: deep time ("4.61 Gyr") over 10,000 years, calendar dates under it, and the hour under 30 days. Ticks follow too, from billions of years down to hours, and sit on the calendar's grid, so "day 3" is a real day boundary.
- **The campaign's now.** The campaign has its own now (`Workspace.campaign_now_yr`), shown as a stamped marker on the track and in the readout ("NOW in the campaign", or "Campaign now: …" when the GM is looking elsewhere). Looking at another time never moves it. While the view sits at the campaign's now, stepping and playing move both: time passes in the campaign. Away from it, "Back to now" returns, and "Make this now" moves the campaign's now to the time being viewed. Players' screens follow the campaign's now (`share.setClock`), not the GM's browsing.
- **Step and play.** Back and Forward step by the chosen unit (hour, day, week, month, year, century, 1,000 years, a million years). Play advances one step a second and sends at most ten updates a second; derivation is quantised to 1,000 years, so play does not hammer the kernel.
- **The track only looks.** Pointer and keyboard on the track (arrows 1 % of the window, Page Up and Down 10 %, Home, End) move the view, never the campaign.
- **Calendar.** Dates are counted from the campaign's start (`campaign_epoch_yr`): "Year 1, day 1" is where the campaign began, not the Big Bang. The default is Earth days and Julian years. A pack can give its own (`Calendar` in `packages/chart-grammar/src/calendar.ts`: day length, year length, optional months, a format such as `{day:03}-{year}` for Traveller). Switching systems keeps the elapsed campaign time.
- **Kept.** The campaign save carries `campaign_now_yr` and `campaign_epoch_yr`; a reload comes back to the same now.
- **Worlds move by the day (part 2).** At every viewed time the shell asks the kernel's `orbits_at` where each body is (two-body Kepler, relative to its host) and lays the phases onto the scene, so the chart, the 3D view and the sound all move together (rendering rule 5). Play asks ten times a second; one call runs at a time and only the latest waits. A fixture scene (Kestrel Reach) sends its own elements: each body's drawn phase becomes its place at the campaign's start (`orbit.epoch_s`), so the chart opens exactly as before and moves from there. A system the kernel holds is placed from its own facts, and a missing phase is a keyed draw labelled `resolved`. A body the kernel cannot place keeps its drawn phase.
- **Pack calendars (part 2).** When a pack gives a calendar (`provides.other.calendar`, kernel `calendar`), the ruler counts dates in it: its epoch, epoch year, day and year length, months and format (for example Traveller's `{day:03}-{year}`, giving 001-1105). It is read again whenever the pack stack changes.

## Different from V3, and why

V3 had separate time sliders and a speed control beside a fixed scale. SSE4 keeps one ruler and zooms it: the same control edits deep time and plays out a week, so there is nothing to switch between, and events stay on it at every scale. V3 had one clock. SSE4 separates the time the GM is looking at from the campaign's now, so the GM can look ahead or back freely while players stay on the campaign's present, and moving the campaign is a deliberate act (stepping at now, or Make this now).

## Precision

The workspace keeps time as f64 years since the origin, which carries about 30 seconds at 14 Gyr. Dates round to whole minutes first (no "11:59" for noon), the finest window is an hour, and `yearsToSeconds` / `secondsToYears` (ui-contract `intent.ts`) keep the fraction of a year to the second and minute.

## Next

- **Players' screens** keep their own chart: phases are not player data there (bodies are spread by order, `PlayerChart.svelte`), so they do not move by the day. If a GM wants players to see where worlds are, the next step is a data-view field for the phase from `orbits_at` at the campaign's clock, granted like any other fact.
- **The campaign's now in the chronicle.** The kernel has `set_campaign_now` (PR 28), but writing it from the GM's machine alone would change the GM's state hash without the players' replicas seeing it, and their link would keep resyncing. It must travel through the shared intent log like any other change: a built-in intent kind in the J5 registry, sent when the campaign's now settles (not at every step of Play). Asked of the kernel thread. Until then the browser save keeps it.
- Times far from the campaign's start: two-body Kepler only (no precession or perturbation), so phases far into deep time are where a perfect clockwork would put them, not a prediction.
