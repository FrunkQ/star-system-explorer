# XO18: the Hazard map (exotic objects design 3.8)

Alex, 24 Nov 2026 17:03 and 17:19: not a list of orbit options but a diagram, for most hazardous bodies ("Jupiter radiation belts and such need to be treated carefully"), with the closest a ship can go before it is "too late" as a figure the GM has and may show the players.

## Part 1 (24 Nov 2026): the reading, `crates/sse-hazard`

`hazard_map(&Ask, &sheet) -> HazardMap`, pure and deterministic, no golden touched (no op, no new block yet).

- **Ask**: the body (mass, radius, own light, optional top of the air and bubble edge), `read_field`'s `belts` for it, the ship (or none: the stated stock ship in `data/hazard.json`), and the line: `equator` (the magnetic equator's plane) or `poles`.
- **Rows**, each in clear, harsh and deadly bands on a 400-point logarithmic grid from the surface to twice the farthest hazard edge:
  - Heat: `sse.climate.insolation` into `sse.climate.equilibrium_temperature` (f = 1/4 for a round hull, 1/2 for a face-on sunshade; albedo 1 − α/ε), against the skin's melting or subliming point from the data sheet (a typed `max_k` only if the sheet has none).
  - Radiation, trapped belts: each shell's dose behind the ship's shield (ln dose across ln shield), zero outside the belts and in each moon's swept lane. At magnetic latitude λ the point is on shell r / cos²λ; over the pole no line closes.
  - Air: a stay inside the air is deadly (top of the air from the belts' inner edge when the air sets it).
  - Climbing out: the escape speed (`sse.planet.escape_velocity`) less the circular speed, against the fuel.
  - Rows that never leave clear are folded into one "nothing to worry about" line.
- **Safe bands** (every span where nothing is deadly), **Park** (the innermost, with the hazard that set it), **Dive** (a parabolic fall-through with its closest point on the line: the closest that comes back, limited by air, heat at the closest point, or the dose integrated over the pass) and the **totals for the pass** (dose, peak hull temperature, hours inside the belts).
- **Marks**: surface, top of the air, the belts' edges and what sets each, each moon's lane (with its width), moons' orbits, the bubble's edge. **Moons**: the belts' dose beside each moon (`read_field`'s `orbits`) and its tone.

Checks (`src/tests.rs`, 7 tests): the Sun with a Parker-like probe dives to 8 to 11 Sun radii, set by heat; a 30 km/s ship parks no closer than 0.15 to 0.19 au; the stock ship's limit is aluminium's melting point from the sheet; Jupiter is deadly beside Io for every shield, harsh beside Europa and Ganymede behind 40 g/cm², clear at Callisto, with a lane at each inner moon; over Jupiter's poles the belts' row folds away and a dive goes deeper than in the plane; Saturn's belts start at the A ring; Earth's air sets the inner edge, a heavier shield parks closer; Venus has no belt row; the same ask gives the same bits.

Honest limits (fudge register, exotic objects section): the dose along a field line is the shell's equatorial dose (an upper bound), so over Jupiter's poles a 10 g/cm² ship dives only to about 12 radii, where Juno passes at about 1.06; the design's Juno check waits for `sse.belts.latitude_falloff`. Belts are electrons only (F40). The tone lines are policy in `data/hazard.json`, with sources. The design's "harsh at Ganymede for a thin shield" does not hold with MF7's belt doses (deadly behind 1 g/cm²); the tests follow the model, and Europa Clipper's per-flyby doses suggest the model is not too high.
