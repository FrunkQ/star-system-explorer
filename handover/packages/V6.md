# V6: System 3D (part 1)

Status: System 3D done (19 Nov 2026), branch `claude/project-thread-adgixl`, in the V4 PR into `main`. Body and Cutaway views are part 2.

## What was built
- **Frames and the camera-relative rule** (`packages/renderer/src/frames.ts`, new renderer package). A tree of frame nodes, each holding its position in its parent's frame in f64 metres.
  - `relative(from, to)` sums only the links below the common ancestor, so two moons of one planet never see the patch's numbers.
  - `project` subtracts the camera in f64, then narrows to float32 and projects in float32, as a GPU does.
  - `projectExact` is the f64 reference.
  - `projectFlattened` is the forbidden way (world positions narrowed, camera subtracted on the GPU). It is kept so the test can show it fails.
  - `rebase` and `frameFor` implement rule 4: the camera is handed to the deepest frame whose span can carry it.
- **GPU path** (`renderer/src/gpu.ts`): three.js 0.187.0 `WebGPURenderer`, which uses WebGL2 when there is no WebGPU.
  - It is loaded lazily as its own chunk (about 800 kB), so pages without 3D never fetch it.
  - It receives camera-relative float32 positions only, with the camera at the origin.
  - The boundary lint now allows the `three` package and checks `packages/renderer/src`.
- **System 3D view** (`views/system-3d`). One frame at a time, in perspective: the centre body and the bodies that orbit it on log-scaled rings. Sizes are not to scale.
  - **Tiers:** GPU (WebGPU, else WebGL2), else Canvas 2D drawing the same projection. The note says which was used and why. The `tier` param can force Canvas 2D.
  - **Controls:** drag or arrow keys turn the camera; the wheel, the + and − buttons or the +/− keys zoom.
  - **Frame handover:** zooming in on a chosen planet with moons hands over to its frame (rule 4), as do "Moons of X ›" and double-click. Zooming out of a planet's frame, "‹ system" or Escape hands back.
  - **Keyboard:** body buttons over the canvas are the keyboard path and carry `data-x`/`data-y` for tests.
  - **Players' scenes:** bodies without a distance are named as "Position not known" and never placed. Phases are not player data, so bodies are spread round their rings, and the note says so.
- **GM**: a 2D/3D switch on the system chart. 3D is view-only; edits stay on the 2D chart. Selection is shared, and choosing a moon opens its planet's frame in either.
- **Players**: `sse.view.system-3d` joins the player view stack. A new screen document, "Bridge in 3D" (`players.bridge-3d.screen.json`), is also offered for seats.

## Acceptance (build plan V6: "each declares tiers; canvas fallback works"; rendering-rules.md: the first 3D view proves pixel stability)
| Criterion | Where it is proved |
|---|---|
| Pixel stability from patch to surface | `renderer/test/frames.test.ts`. The camera looks at a lander on a moon's surface while it zooms from 3×10^21 m to 10 m, in quarter-decade steps, through every frame (patch, star, planet, moon, lander). The lander's exact pixel moves under 1 px across the whole sweep. The float32 camera-relative path stays within 0.0001 px of it. The flattened path is off by more than 100 px or drops off screen, so the test has teeth |
| Each view declares tiers | `sse.view.system-3d` declares `gpu-basic` and `canvas`, with cost per option (auto: medium, canvas: light) under the V11 budget |
| Canvas fallback works | `tests/ui/system3d.e2e.mjs` runs twice in Chromium, once normally (WebGL2 here) and once with WebGL and 3D APIs disabled (Canvas 2D). Both draw the GM's 3D chart and the players' 3D screen, hand over to Vault's frame (Hasp, Rivet, Latch) and back by zooming out, and turn the camera from the keyboard |

Results: 82 UI node tests pass; svelte-check clean; `scripts/check.sh` passes; all eight e2e suites pass in Chromium. Screens: `V6-screens/`.

## Not done, and why
- **Body and Cutaway views** (part 2). Body needs the planet layer (surface design, height map), and Cutaway needs interior shells from the kernel. Neither is in scenes yet.
- **Still and gpu-high tiers, and the frame-time governor**: they come with the Body view, which is where they matter.
- **Reversed depth**: not needed at display scale (one frame, display units of 0.1 to 60). It is needed once a view draws true-scale distances.
- **True-scale mode**: the frames module carries true metres (proved by the test), but the view draws log-scaled display distances, as the 2D chart does.
- **Orbit tilt and eccentricity**: scenes carry `a` and phase only, so rings are circles in the orbital plane.
