# V6: System 3D (part 1)

Status: System 3D done (19 Nov 2026), branch `claude/project-thread-adgixl`, in the V4 PR into `main`. Body and Cutaway views are part 2.

## What was built
- **Frames and the camera-relative rule** (`packages/renderer/src/frames.ts`, new renderer package). A tree of frame nodes, each holding its position in its parent's frame in f64 metres.
  - `relative(from, to)` sums only the links below the common ancestor, so two moons of one planet never see the patch's numbers.
  - `project` subtracts the camera in f64, then narrows to float32 and projects in float32, as a GPU does.
  - `projectExact` is the f64 reference.
  - `projectFlattened` is the forbidden way (world positions narrowed, camera subtracted on the GPU). It is kept so the test can show it fails.
  - `rebase` and `frameFor` implement rule 4: the camera is handed to the deepest frame whose span can carry it.
- **GPU path** (`renderer/src/gpu.ts`): three.js 0.187.0 `WebGPURenderer`, which uses WebGL2 when there is no WebGPU.
  - It is loaded lazily as its own chunk (about 800 kB), so pages without 3D never fetch it.
  - It receives camera-relative float32 positions only, with the camera at the origin.
  - The boundary lint now allows the `three` package and checks `packages/renderer/src`.
- **System 3D view** (`views/system-3d`). One frame at a time, in perspective: the centre body and the bodies that orbit it on log-scaled rings. Sizes are not to scale.
  - **Tiers:** GPU (WebGPU, else WebGL2), else Canvas 2D drawing the same projection. The note says which was used and why. The `tier` param can force Canvas 2D.
  - **Controls:** drag or arrow keys turn the camera; the wheel, the + and − buttons or the +/− keys zoom.
  - **Frame handover:** zooming in on a chosen planet with moons hands over to its frame (rule 4), as do "Moons of X ›" and double-click. Zooming out of a planet's frame, "‹ system" or Escape hands back.
  - **Keyboard:** body buttons over the canvas are the keyboard path and carry `data-x`/`data-y` for tests.
  - **Players' scenes:** bodies without a distance are named as "Position not known" and never placed. Phases are not player data, so bodies are spread round their rings, and the note says so.
- **GM**: a 2D/3D switch on the system chart. 3D is view-only; edits stay on the 2D chart. Selection is shared, and choosing a moon opens its planet's frame in either.
- **Players**: `sse.view.system-3d` joins the player view stack. A new screen document, "Bridge in 3D" (`players.bridge-3d.screen.json`), is also offered for seats.

## Not done, and why
- **Body and Cutaway views** (part 2). Body needs the planet layer (surface design, height map), and Cutaway needs interior shells from the kernel. Neither is in scenes yet.
- **Still and gpu-high tiers, and the frame-time governor**: they come with the Body view, which is where they matter.
- **Reversed depth**: not needed at display scale (one frame, display units of 0.1 to 60). It is needed once a view draws true-scale distances.
- **True-scale mode**: the frames module carries true metres (proved by the test), but the view draws log-scaled display distances, as the 2D chart does.
- **Orbit tilt and eccentricity**: scenes carry `a` and phase only, so rings are circles in the orbital plane.

## Part 3: Cutaway (19 Nov, UI track)

What it does: a new **Body** screen (`documents/body.gm.json`) puts `sse.view.cutaway` in the chart area, with the body card beside it and the ruler below. It shows the selected world.

- **The cut.** A wedge (140° by default, `wedge_deg` 60 to 360) through the world. Global shells fill the wedge. Shells that lie only under a region (Tarn's polar ice shell and sub-ice brine ocean, `within: south-pole`) lie over them in a sector at the wedge's edge, named for the region. Features sit on the centre line at their heights. The surface's features get one mark, worded like the ladder's surface line. Stations above every shell sit in the margin above the cut. Labels alternate sides so neighbours do not overprint. Boundary ticks run down the left edge.
- **Scale.** `scale` "log" (default) gives the air above the surface the outer fifth of the radius and spreads depth below on a log scale, so a 3 km ice lid shows on a 6,090 km world. "true" keeps proportions; the lid is then under a pixel. The caption says which is in use.
- **The ladder.** "Above, on and within" beside the cut, worded by `ladder()` in `ui-contract/src/world.ts`. That is a line-for-line port of sse-world's `WorldStack::ladder()`, and `world.test.ts` checks it against the same Strata table as `crates/sse-world/src/tests.rs`. Clicking a line marks its shell or feature on the cut.
- **No stack, no picture.** A body with no world stack says "no cutaway yet" and lists the worlds that have one. Nothing is invented.
- Looking inside changes nothing: the state hash holds (e2e).

Data (updated 19 Nov, after the physics track's `read_world_stack`, merged from PR 4 head ac61f49). The view asks the kernel for the selected body's stack by address; when the kernel has none (no stack built from history yet) it asks again by the P4 fixture the campaign names for that body. That link is `documents/kestrel.worlds.json` (`sse.world-links/0`: Tarn → `tarn`), carried on the scene as `SceneSubject.world_fixture`. Stack, ladder and physics notes all come from the kernel; the view says when the stack is a fixture ("The kernel serves this stack from its P4 fixture …"). Picking a ladder line shows the kernel's physics notes for it (the brine ocean: "freezes at 265.1 K … at 266 K it is liquid"), and any physics problems show under the cut. When the surface track builds stacks (S7 on), the first request (by address) returns them and the link file can go.

Checks: `ui-contract/test/world.test.ts` (Strata ladder; the kernel serves all six fixtures, each valid, and the TS `ladder()` port words each exactly as the kernel's ladder does; Kestrel's Tarn has no built stack and its link resolves; km words; cut radii in order on both scales and the log lid visible). The e2e also checks the source is `fixture:tarn` and the brine ocean's physics note. `tests/ui/cutaway.e2e.mjs` (pick Tarn from the empty screen; 6 shells, 2 under the south pole; 9 ladder lines with the kernel's words; a body without a stack says so; state hash unchanged; no sideways scroll at 390 px). Added to `npm run ui:e2e` and CI (Chromium and Firefox). Screens: `V6-screens/cutaway-gm.png`, `cutaway-phone.png`.

- **Phones.** The screen picker is hidden at phone width, as for Symbols and Styles, so phones reach the Body screen only by link or test. A phone screen menu is a polish item for all of them.

## Part 4: Body globe and the phone screen menu (19 Nov, UI track)

What it does: the **Body** screen now puts `sse.view.body` in the chart area. It draws the selected world as a globe from the kernel's S1 surface tiles, states the surface in words, and mounts the Cutaway (part 3) below it (`cutaway` param, on by default; the Cutaway's own params pass through).

- **Data.** `Session.surfaceTiles(system, body)` asks `resolved_system` for the six level-0 tiles of one body (one per cube face; GM only). The physics track's atlas (`crates/sse-surface/src/atlas.rs`) builds them from the world's history; the view only draws them. About 1 to 2 s per world in the browser, so tiles are cached per system and body for the page's life.
- **The globe.** Orthographic, on a 2D canvas (tier `canvas`; no GPU needed), `size_px` 160 to 640. Each pixel: view to world direction (`viewToWorld`), the face and sample it falls in (`faceOf`, `sampleAt`, the same FACES and tangent warp as the kernel's `direction` and `face_of`), then a colour (`sampleColour`: water by depth, magma glowing, hydrocarbon dark, bare ground by material, height and albedo), lit from the upper left. Drag or arrow keys turn it (Shift for 45°).
- **In words.** Share under each cover, highest and lowest ground, main bare materials, the kernel's regime label, the epoch, the tiles' `pending` list ("not yet modelled": flow and channels S3, feature tags, biome S11, structures S7), sample count and level. The same sentence is the canvas's accessible name, so the text tier reads it.
- **No tiles, no globe.** A star says the view is for worlds. A body with no surface (Kestrel's fixture worlds, whose facts carry no history) says "No surface for Tarn yet" and how to get one (grow or import a system with Make). Its Cutaway still shows.
- **Phone screen menu.** At phone width the screen tabs were hidden, so Body, Symbols, Styles, Packs and Player data were unreachable. A "Screen menu" select now replaces the phone's chart/physics toggle and reaches every screen. On a phone, tool screens get a taller scrolling window (72% of the height), and the flow editor and the Body view flow with the page.

Code: `packages/ui-contract/src/globe.ts` (pure maths and words), `packages/views/body/` (module and `Body.svelte`), `Session.surfaceTiles`, `documents/body.gm.json`, App's screen menu.

Checks: `ui-contract/test/globe.test.ts` (direction and faceOf round trip on all six faces; the view's centre faces the turned longitude; the sample Sol adopted into the campaign gives Earth six tiles, a regime, 40%+ under water, ground above and below sea level, and each sample's centre falls back in that sample; Tarn has no tiles; players are refused). `tests/ui/globe.e2e.mjs` (Tarn says it has no surface but keeps its Cutaway; add the sample Sol, pick Earth, open Body: canvas drawn (centre opaque, corner clear), 71% under water, regime named, arrow keys turn it and redraw; on a 390 px phone the screen menu reaches Body and Symbols with no sideways scroll). `shell.e2e.mjs` uses the menu for the phone's physics screen. Added to `npm run ui:e2e` and CI. Screens: `V6-screens/globe-gm.png`, `globe-phone.png`.

### Still open
- **Finer tiles.** Only level 0 is drawn (about 256 by 256 samples a face, a few tens of km at Earth's size). Zooming in should ask for level 1 and on, by the tiles in view.
- **GPU tier and relief shading.** The canvas globe is flat-lit per sample; a gpu-basic tier with slope shading from `slope_ddeg` would look better and be faster.
- **Fixture worlds.** Kestrel's worlds get surfaces once the campaign's own system has history (adopted or grown), or the kernel builds surfaces for fixture worlds.
- **Phone title.** With the screen menu, the system's name is squeezed out of the phone's top bar; it shows in the card.
