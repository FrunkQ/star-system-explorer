# S16a: detail levels, part 1 (Orbit and Survey are one world)

Design: `handover/design/SSE4-rock3-comparison.md` section 5.8 (accepted by Alex on 21 Nov, "agree with all
proposals"). Build order there: S16a first, then S14a lava tubes, S15 impactor provenance, S2b, S9 and S10,
S14b and map export.

## The problem it fixes (confirmed in code)
The globe shown for a world nobody had surveyed was the S0 regime sketch: keyed smooth bumps, under
`shell:sketch@`. The surveyed world was S2's plate run, under `shell:surface@`. Nothing tied the two, so a
world's continents changed when it was surveyed. Two free plate runs at different grids were no better: the
Orbit-sized run and the surveyed run agreed on land and sea for only 68% of Earth.

## What changed
- `sse-surface/src/detail.rs` (new): `Level` (Glance, Orbit, Survey, Site) with the badge words and the
  "below resolution" why-answer for each, `orbit_settings`, `orbit_key` (world, code version, level, epoch)
  and `orbit_atlas`.
  - For a world that ever had moving plates, the Orbit atlas holds heights from the coarse plate run, under
    `shell:orbit@`.
  - For one that never did, it is the sketch at every level, under `shell:sketch@` (unchanged).
- `plates.rs`:
  - `PlateSettings.guide` (the base pack sets 32 cells a face, 25 Myr) names the coarse run. With a guide,
    `history` steps the coarse run and moves the fine grid with the same plates inside each coarse step. It
    then pins the fine grid to the coarse one (`pin`):
    - plates, poles, speeds and every dated event are the coarse run's;
    - a fine cell inside a coarse cell whose neighbours all share its plate and crust kind takes them;
    - near a coarse coast, the fine cell keeps its own shape only where the coarse land share around it is
      between 0.4 and 0.6;
    - floods the coarse run stamps are stamped on the fine grid too.
  - `PlateHistory.guided_by` records the coarse grid. A guide no coarser than the run is ignored, so the
    16-a-face golden still runs free.
  - Every history now ends exactly on its epoch, with one shorter last step, and the time ruler's last
    snapshot is the epoch.
  - New sea floor is dated within the step: each cell is traced back along its plate's turn to where it
    leaves the plate's old ground (8 samples).
  - Boundaries are classified by the motion of the last step, before that step's pole jumps.
  - Speed:
    - `Grid` keeps its neighbours and finds cells by comparing tangents, with no arctangents;
    - each step tests only the plates whose forward-turned cells land beside a cell;
    - the previous cells are borrowed, not cloned.
    - These made the surveyed Earth run go from about 3.7 s to 1.4 s natively.
- Kernel:
  - `resolved_system` tiles (what the Body view's globe reads) come from the Orbit atlas. Each open universe
    keeps the last 8 in a cache keyed by `orbit_key`, so reopening a world reuses its run. A world whose
    tiles nobody asks for costs nothing: a click or a list never starts the plate run.
  - `survey_surface` and `plan_descent` with `quick` now run the Orbit level. Before, they ran a separate
    16-a-face grid, which was a third, different world.
- `PLATES_VERSION` is now `surface-plates-v2`.
- Fudges F rows "`plates.rs` `pin`" and "ridge dating" are in `docs/fudges.md` under Surfaces.

## Checks
- `plates_tests::the_surveyed_world_refines_the_orbit_world` (Earth):
  - the surveyed run has the Orbit run's events and plates;
  - every fine cell that disagrees with its coarse cell lies on a coarse coast or plate edge, so coasts
    move by at most one coarse cell;
  - land and sea agree on at least 95% of fine cells and at least 95% averaged back to the coarse grid
    (measured: 96% and 97%);
  - a free fine run agrees less.
- `detail_tests::the_orbit_globe_is_the_surveyed_world`: land and sea from the Orbit atlas against the full
  survey (wear, impacts, landforms and cover on top) agree on at least 90% of a 48-a-face grid. The old
  sketch globe agrees less.
- Worlds without plates (Mars, Venus, Moon) keep one sketch at every level. Orbit keys and tiles are
  reproducible.
- Crust ages still grow away from ridges. The near-ridge mean is under 0.6 of the mean 4 to 8 cells out.
- Cost on Earth, natively (release): Orbit run about 0.2 s (6,144 cells, 176 steps); surveyed run about
  1.4 s. The doc's estimate of 100 Myr Orbit steps was tried and rejected: steps that long move plates about
  60° each and leave ridges dated 140 Myr old. The wasm figure on the harness is still to measure.

## Goldens re-blessed on purpose
The plate history changed (pinning, ridge dating, the exact last step), so every golden built on it moved.
- Surface goldens: `surface-plates`, `surface-wear`, `surface-cover`.
- Kernel contract lines that carry surveyed or quick surfaces.

## Part 2: the whole chain at Orbit level
- `survey::orbit_world`: every surface stage but the feature register, on the Orbit grid. This covers plates,
  wear, impacts, regime landforms, cover and climate. Only craters and landforms at least two coarse cells
  across are kept (620 km on an Earth); smaller ones are left to the texture. Plate worlds' atlases are under
  `shell:orbit@`.
- `detail::orbit_atlas(s, body, seed, epoch)` now runs it. The kernel's Body-globe tiles therefore show worn
  relief, the big craters and basins, ice and climate colour at Orbit level, not bare plate heights.
- Worlds without plates (Mars, Venus, Moon): Orbit and surveyed heights correlate above 0.8 on the six
  level-0 tiles (`detail_tests::a_world_without_plates_is_one_world_at_every_level`).
- Speed:
  - `regimes.rs` dated each landform by bisection over a 400-step integral, 16,000 steps per landform. It
    now tabulates the renewal integral once (1,024 steps) and inverts it.
  - On Venus and Io the landform stage went from 0.5 s to 2 ms, at Survey level too.
  - Golden `surface-regimes` re-blessed on purpose: landform dates move slightly with the finer integral.
- Cost natively (release), chain only: 0.1 to 0.3 s per world. Earth is the slowest, because of its plate
  run.
- Level-0 tiles at 256 samples cost 0.15 to 0.45 s each, against about 0.1 s for the old bare sketch.
  Opening a Body globe (six tiles) is therefore about 1 to 3 s natively the first time. Tiles are not
  cached; the atlas is.

## Not in this part (later S16 work)
- Climate bands are those of S7's cover at Orbit resolution; no separate Orbit climate model.
- Named features at Orbit level: the register still runs only on survey.
- A tile cache for the Body globe's level-0 tiles, if the 1 to 3 s first open is too slow on the harness.
- Reuse beyond this device: fetching an Orbit or Survey result from the GM's machine, a peer or a pack.
  This needs shell and session work.
- The resolution control (Glance, Orbit, Survey, Site on the Body view, and the device cap) and the quality
  badge. The words are in `Level::badge`; the control is chart room shell work.
- Per-device cache budgets in browser storage (OPFS), and the scripted 50-world click-through check that no
  Survey work runs.
- S16b, the fine run at 128 a face, comes with S2b. The pinning here is already the mechanism it needs.
