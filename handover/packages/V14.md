# V14: Size comparison and measuring (UI track)

Status: done 19 Nov 2026. A new package from Alex's ask on 19 Nov: V3's size comparison and measuring, which are among V3's most used tools, kept as a GM view (`docs/v3-parity.md`: "Size comparison, as a GM and player view: Keep"). It also lets you pull in bodies from anywhere in the campaign, and stock reference objects.

## What it does

**Sizes screen** (`documents/compare.gm.json`, tab "Sizes", also in the phone screen menu). `sse.view.compare` sits in the chart area, with the body card beside it and the ruler below.

- **The lineup.** Bodies stand side by side on one baseline at one true scale. It starts with this system's star and worlds in orbit order. Each system keeps its own lineup for the page's life, so it survives moving between screens.
- **The limb.** When the largest body is more than `limb_ratio` (default 5) times the next, it is drawn as a limb entering from the left, as on a size-comparison poster. The scale is then set by the rest. A star beside its planets reads at once. The limb is at the same true scale, and its label says "edge only".
- **Nothing dropped.** A body under a pixel at the current scale is drawn at 1.5 px inside a dashed ring and labelled "under a pixel". A hint suggests Log scale or removing the largest. Each item takes at least a label's width, so labels never overprint.
- **Log scale.** Radius grows with log10(km), so a neutron star and Betelgeuse can stand together. It is marked "Not to scale" on the picture and in its accessible name.
- **Adding.** "From <system>" offers this system's bodies, including moons. "From the campaign" lists every other system the kernel holds (`systems`, then `resolved_system` for each), grouped by system. "Reference objects" lists the stock objects. "Surprise me" adds a random body from any of these. "By size", "Start again" (this system's own worlds) and "Clear" are there too.
- **Reference objects** (`documents/reference-objects.json`, `sse.reference-objects/0`). There is one of each standard planet type: iron-rich (Mercury), small rocky (Mars), rocky with oceans (Earth), sub-Neptune (K2-18 b), ice giant (Neptune), ringed and plain gas giants (Saturn, Jupiter) and brown dwarf (typical). Moons and small worlds are Ceres, Europa, the Moon and Titan. Star types are M (Proxima), K (Alpha Centauri B), G (Sun), F (Procyon A), A (Sirius A), a K giant (Arcturus), a B supergiant (Rigel) and an M supergiant (Betelgeuse). Remnants are a white dwarf (Sirius B) and a typical neutron star. Each object has a measured mean radius and a reference with a link, or is marked as a typical value. `checkReferences` rejects a document with a bad radius, unknown group or unlinked reference. A pack can ship its own list later (the module's `references` param names the document).
- **The table.** It lists body, type, where it comes from, radius in km, size in words ("0.95 × Earth", "1.02 × Jupiter", "764 × Sun"), and what the radius rests on: the fact's evidence (fixture, set, resolved, derived), or "measured" / "typical value" linking to the reference. Each row has a remove button. Selecting a body from this system (click its disc or name) opens its card, and the table then gives every size as a multiple of it.
- **Colour.** Planets take the symbology's colour and pattern (colour-coded skins colour by class). Stars take a rough blackbody colour from their surface temperature. This is presentation only.
- **Honesty.** Radii come only from the bodies' own radius facts (`radius_earth`, `radius_sun`). A body without one is not offered, and the panel says how many. Nothing is estimated in the view.

**Measure on the chart.** A "Measure" button sits with the zoom controls. Pick two bodies and the chart draws a dashed line between them. A panel gives the distance now (AU and km, or km under 0.01 AU) and the light time. When both circle the same parent, it also gives the closest and farthest they can be. It says this comes from the orbits as drawn (circular, in one plane, at the ruler's time), and that chart distances are on a log scale while these are true. It works within a planet's frame of moons too. Measuring changes nothing: no edit, no selection. Pressing Measure again ends it.

## Checks

- `ui-contract/test/compare.test.ts` checks:
  - the reference document is valid and has every planet type and the M to B star types;
  - items come only from radius facts;
  - one true scale with tiny bodies marked and slots kept apart;
  - the limb with the scale set by the rest, and no limb between similar stars;
  - log mode is ordered;
  - size words, scale bar and star colours;
  - measure: star to Tarn is Tarn's orbit, light time, a pair within its closest and farthest, and the distance and light words.
- `tests/ui/compare.e2e.mjs` checks:
  - Measure: Kestrel to Tarn "0.52 AU (77.8 million km), 4.3 light minutes", Vault to Tarn with closest and farthest, the line drawn and cleared;
  - Sizes starts with Kestrel's six bodies, with Tarn's row in words;
  - Jupiter and a neutron star come in, and the neutron star is "under a pixel";
  - log scale is marked not to scale with nothing tiny;
  - remove works, and selecting Tarn gives multiples of Tarn;
  - the lineup survives a screen change;
  - after adding the sample Sol, its Earth comes in from the campaign, and Surprise me adds one more;
  - on a phone the screen menu reaches Sizes with no sideways scroll.
- Both are in `npm run ui:e2e` and CI (Chromium and Firefox).

## Not done, and why

- **Reference values need checking.** The radii and references in `reference-objects.json` were written from memory, like other citations in this project. The planets are NASA fact-sheet values. The star and remnant radii and their DOIs should be checked against the papers before release.
- **Players' version.** V3 showed sizes to players too. A player lens needs the player scene's sizes, which carry only what the table knows; it will come with the player view work. The GM can show the lineup on a shared screen meanwhile.
- **Measuring free points.** Measure is body to body. Measuring from an arbitrary point (a ship's position, a jump point) waits for positions that are not bodies (T3 routes).
- **Eccentric and inclined orbits.** The scene carries only the semi-major axis and phase, so closest and farthest assume circular coplanar orbits, and the panel says so. When scenes carry full elements, the numbers can use them.
- **More reference sets.** Packs (Traveller, a universe pack) could ship their own lists: stock ships, famous stations, landmarks. The document format is ready for it, but there is no pack loader for it yet.
