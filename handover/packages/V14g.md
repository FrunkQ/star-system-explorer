# V14g: the size comparison as turning globes

Status: done 24 Nov 2026. From Alex's ask on 24 Nov: "will size comparison get 3d, true and wireframe in time, slowly spinning?"

## What it does

The Sizes screen now offers all four view styles. **Chart** is the flat lineup of discs, as before. **3D**, **True** and **Wireframe** stand the same lineup up as globes. The places, the sizes, the limb, the labels, the scale bar and the picking stay with the lineup's own drawing (`layoutLineup`). Only the discs are swapped for globes, drawn on the GPU (`Lineup3D.svelte`, in front of nothing and behind the labels).

- **Each body turns on its own axis.** It leans by its own tilt (`tilt_rad` on the scene subject; for a reference body, `body.obliquity` in base-sol). It turns its own way: a tilt past a right angle (Venus, Uranus, Pluto) turns backwards as seen from its orbit's north, as it truly does. A negative day also turns backwards.
- **A pace that can be watched.** By default every body makes one turn in 40 seconds (`SECONDS_PER_TURN`). **True pace** keeps the real proportions: the quickest in the lineup makes one turn in 40 seconds and the rest go in proportion, and the line under the lineup says how many hours of turning one second shows. A body whose day is not on record stands still, and the line says so. Under reduced motion the globes stand still.
- **3D**: the globe is shaded in its chart colour, lit from the upper left on the chart's ground. A graticule is drawn over it, trimmed to the near side (meridians and parallels every 30°, with the equator and one meridian in the accent colour so the turn reads). Rings are drawn as their edges.
- **Wireframe**: the graticule alone, see-through, and the rings' edges.
- **True**: lit from the side against black space. A world of this system shows its own surface where the kernel can read it (GM seat, `surfaceTiles`, read once per world for the page's life). A world with no readable surface is plain grey, as in the system's 3D. Rings are drawn from their profile (`ringProfile`: τ and albedo where known). Stars shine by their own colour.
- **Seen from** tips every globe together over their orbits' plane, the row staying put: Edge-on (poles up the screen), Tilted (rings open), Above (poles toward you). **Free**: drag to turn them all. Picking a fixed view frames it afresh.
- **Room for rings.** In the globe styles a ringed body takes the width of its rings at the same scale (`layoutLineup(..., withRings)`, `reachKm`), so Saturn's rings never cross a neighbour.
- **Reference bodies turn too.** A Solar System reference object names its row in the base-sol pack (`"sol": "saturn"`), which gives it its measured day, tilt and rings. The Sun's day is the sheet's `P_rot_sun`. Nothing is typed into the reference document.

The camera is orthographic (`ortho` on `Gpu.draw`), so sizes are the lineup's whatever the depth. A huge limb stays the flat drawing: a sphere thousands of pixels across would need a finer mesh than it earns.

## Gallery (V14h, 24 Nov), then taken out

Alex first asked for "a gallery of the size comparison tool", then put it where he meant it: "the gallery needs to show every type on the classifier screen ... and can control reasonable numbers on the screen". The Sizes screen's Gallery button was taken out again; the class gallery is V14i on Reference, Classes (see `handover/status/renderer.md`).

What stays from V14h: the faces come from one door, `trueLook` in `packages/renderer/src/true-body.ts` (surface, rings, a star's own light; the ring profile cache and `meanColour` moved there from System3D). System3D's True and the lineup's True both call it, so a look the other builders add there (giants' bands, hazes, plumes, frosts, a star's glow) shows in every True view with no second code path. The workspace's `pickView` (App.svelte) lets a view set the viewer's choice.

What a body needs for a real face: a world of the shown system needs its surface tiles from the kernel (GM seat), read once per world; until then it is plain grey. A reference object has no kernel body: grey unless a look in `trueLook` can be made from its data.
