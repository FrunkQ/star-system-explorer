# Y5 part 1: the night sky drawn (the stars beyond)

Night sky design (`design/SSE4-night-sky-design.md`), stage Y5, renderer side. The kernel's Y2 (`sky_from_here`)
and Y3 (`sky_glow`) give two halves of one sky. The stars that reach the limit come as points, with direction,
magnitudes in every band, temperature, distance and address. Everyone else's light comes as a HEALPix glow map per
band. Drawn together, they add up to the whole sky without counting any star twice.

## What was built
- `packages/ui-contract/src/session.ts`: `session.skyFromHere(ask)` and `session.skyGlow(ask)`, both read only.
- `packages/renderer/src/sky.ts`:
  - **Reading.** `readSky(stars, glow)` builds one sky: stars faintest first with their magnitude in every band,
    and the glow as magnitudes and as linear light.
  - **HEALPix.** RING `ang2pixRing` and `pix2angRing` are written fresh from Górski et al. 2005 (ApJ 622, 759).
    `glowSample` blends neighbouring pixel centres in linear light, so the pixel diamonds do not show.
  - **Camera.** `skyCamera`, `projectSky` and `unprojectSky` form a stereographic camera in galactic coordinates,
    with the galactic north up.
  - **Display.** It is in the fudge register:
    - `glowLevel` and `exposureFor`: V at 18 mag per square arcsecond is white, and other bands use the exposure a
      camera set for that band would use.
    - `pointSpread`: each star's flux spreads as a Gaussian, so brighter stars show wider.
    - V is actual colour: stars by their temperature, the glow by its B−V (Ballesteros 2012). Other bands are grey
      and labelled "Mapped".
  - **Drawing.** `drawSky` draws the glow at a coarse grid with bilinear filling, then each star added on top. The
    point budget follows the design ruling (`SKY_POINTS_MAX`: 20,000 on canvas, 200,000 on GPU tiers); the
    brightest are kept, and the words say how many fell to the glow.
  - **Pick and words.** `pickStar` finds the star under a click. `narrateSky` gives the count, the three brightest
    with their distances (light time), and the glow's whole-sky magnitude.
- `packages/views/window/src/SkyBeyond.svelte`, mounted at the foot of the Window ("Sky from here"):
  - "Show the night sky from here" opens it.
  - The band buttons switch between Blue, Visible and Near infrared. The stars are chosen once in V, so a switch
    redraws without new reads.
  - Field slider, drag to turn, and "Toward the galaxy's centre".
  - A click on a star shows its magnitude, distance, temperature and luminosity.
  - It is seen from the system's place, or from the Sun's place, with a note, when the system has no place in the
    galaxy.

## Not yet lined up with the planets
The Window's bodies are in the system's own frame; the sky is galactic. The kernel thread is adding each system's
orientation (`frame: {pole_l_deg, pole_b_deg, node_l_deg, to_galactic}` in window_view and resolved_system). Sol
gets the measured IAU pole from the master sheet; other systems are drawn isotropically and marked "drawn". Until
that lands, the sky is its own panel and says so.

## Evidence
- `node --test packages/renderer/test/sky.test.ts` passes 5 of 5:
  - every HEALPix pixel centre maps back to its pixel at nside 1, 2, 4 and 16, and the pixels have equal areas
    within 5%;
  - the camera's inverse undoes it and the field edge sits at half the field;
  - Ballesteros gives about 5,800 K for B−V 0.65;
  - on a fixture, the plane is brighter than high latitudes, a star behind the camera is not drawn, a click finds a
    star, and a budget of one keeps the brightest;
  - the kernel's sky from the Sun's place at magnitude 3: every star is within the limit, and the glow toward the
    centre is at least 1 mag per square arcsecond brighter than at the pole.
- `tests/ui/window.e2e.mjs` (Y5 block), on Kestrel:
  - 1,839 stars to magnitude 5, with the band across the middle brighter than high latitudes;
  - a click on the brightest drawn point picks a star;
  - the near-infrared switch redraws with no new read, and the state hash is unchanged.
- Screenshots in `Y5-screens/`. In visible light, the band has a dark dust lane along its middle. In near infrared,
  the lane is gone and the band is smooth: dust is clear at 2 µm, as the design asks.

## Kernel follow-ups (reported to the kernel thread, which is taking them)
- `sky_from_here` takes 46 s at magnitude 6.5 (5.4 s at 4). The kernel aims for about 10 times faster, so the panel
  uses magnitude 5 for now.
- The top luminosity class drew a 4 × 10⁷ L☉ cool supergiant. It is being capped near the Humphreys–Davidson
  limit.
