# S5: hazard layer and descent scene (19 Nov 2026)

Surface design row: "Hazard layer (slope, roughness, weather) and the descent scene from NAV"; checks "descent
physics against known cases (thin-air Mars landing needs engines; Titan parachute descent is long); the reference
clip's content reproduced from a fixture". Needs S4 and T3. Physics track.

## What was built
- **Descent physics** (`crates/sse-nav/src/descent.rs`), pack data under `nav.descent` in `packs/base-nav`.
  - Planar entry with RK4 at 0.1 s: altitude, speed, flight-path angle, downrange; inverse-square gravity and the
    curvature term.
  - **Heating**: Sutton–Graves stagnation-point heating q = k √(ρ/r_n) v³, with k by gas (N2-O2, CO2, N2-CH4,
    H2-He; Sutton and Graves 1971, NASA TR R-376). Peak heating and the heat load; over the shield limit is
    `burned-up`.
  - **Lift**: a lift-to-drag ratio flown at a fixed bank (`lift_to_drag`, `bank_deg`). No bank guidance yet.
  - **Parachute**: deploys below its Mach and dynamic-pressure limits, with a fill time (area grows as (t/fill)²,
    after Knacke 1992).
  - **Engines**: they light once the air no longer does most of the braking (drag past its peak and below full
    thrust), when the stopping distance at 80% of the spare thrust (times `burn_margin`) reaches the ground. Under a
    parachute they wait for the vertical stopping distance, then release the chute and back shell (`jettison_kg`).
    Guidance is two-axis: a descent-rate profile w² = w₀² + 1.6·spare·z with the steady deceleration fed forward, and
    the rest of the thrust on killing the sideways speed, holding height while that takes time (an Apollo-style
    powered descent, simplified).
  - **Outcome**: landed, hard landing (up to 3 × safe speed), crash, burned up, or still descending (a skip-out);
    touchdown speed, duration, peak g, peak heating and heat load, propellant used, downrange, the chute's own
    terminal speed at the ground, the method (chute, engines, chute-and-engines, aerobrake-only), whether engines
    are needed, and `why` lines. Events (entry, peak heating, chute, burn, chute released, out of fuel, touchdown,
    heat shield) and telemetry every 10 s (altitude, speed, vertical speed, downrange, g, heating, phase, throttle):
    the content the reference clip's sidebar needs.
  - **Hazards** share one shape, `Hazard {kind, level (ok, caution, danger), source, why}`, so new hazard blocks add
    entries without changing readers. The descent adds heating, deceleration and touchdown.
  - **Stock landers**: crew capsule (Soyuz-like), heavy Mars lander (MSL-like: lift 0.24, chute, 26 kN descent stage,
    1000 kg shell jettisoned), Titan probe (Huygens-like), airless lander (Apollo LM-like).
- **Site hazards** (`crates/sse-surface/src/site.rs`): read from the one height map at the atlas level that resolves
  a third of the site radius (2 km by default).
  - Slope from a least-squares plane through 25 samples (centre and three rings of eight), plus the steepest step at
    the finest spacing; roughness as the RMS about the plane; relief.
  - Below the datum on a world with surface liquid, the site is a splashdown.
  - Limits (slope 15°, roughness 5 m) are pack data.
- **Kernel op `plan_descent`** (`crates/sse-kernel/src/descent.rs`): surveys the world through the same cache as
  `survey_surface` (fixture, or a body of an adopted system at `at_s`), judges the site, builds the air from the
  survey's air profile (ρ = p M / (R T), sound speed from γ = cp / (cp − R/M)) and flies the lander. Default entry:
  the top of the air (density under 1e-9 kg/m³) at circular speed and −2° (`entry_fpa_deg`), or 15 km above the
  site at orbital speed on an airless world. Response `descent_planned {descent}` with body, source, lander, entry,
  site, air at the site, gravity, hazards, verdict (`landed`, `landed-on-a-bad-site`, or the descent's result) and
  the descent. Errors `unknown_lander`, `no_ground` (a giant), `bad_request`, plus the survey's. Heavy cost (the
  survey; the descent itself is under 0.1 s).
- `survey.rs` gained `surveyed()`, the survey-or-cache step both ops share.

## Checks
- `cargo test -p sse-nav descent` (isothermal air, no survey):
  - Earth capsule from 120 km at 7.6 km/s lands on its parachute at 4 to 10 m/s, 2 to 10 g, 20 to 400 W/cm²;
  - Mars: without engines it crashes (the chute alone gives over 40 m/s, so engines are needed); with them it lands
    on chute and engines;
  - Titan: the probe takes over 1.5 hours under its chute and lands under 7 m/s;
  - Moon: lands on engines from a 15 km low orbit, using a Δv between the orbital speed and 1.5 × it.
- `cargo test -p sse-kernel plan_descent` (quick fixture surveys):
  - Earth's site at 45° N 60° E is at sea: a splashdown, flagged as a liquid danger;
  - **Mars lands at a low site (0°, 120°, 39 m) and crashes on high ground (0°, 180°, 5.5 km)**, where the air is too
    thin for the chute to open in time. That is why real Mars landers go to low basins; nothing was tuned to make it so;
  - Moon on engines; Titan long; deterministic; refusals.
- **Golden**: `tests/golden/nav.json` gains `descents` (the four isothermal cases) on purpose, for S5. The route cases
  are unchanged.
- **Contract**: four new cases (Titan, Moon, Jupiter refused, unknown lander). `contract.jsonl` was re-blessed: the
  hello op list and the unknown-op message gain `plan_descent`, plus the new lines. TS was regenerated.

## Known limits and next steps
- **No bank guidance**: a fixed bank. Direct hyperbolic entries (e.g. MSL's 5.8 km/s at −15.5°) need bank
  modulation, so the test flies from orbit.
- **Wind** is a field only (0): weather (W track) supplies winds and storms as hazards with source `weather`.
- **More hazards to plug in** (Alex, 19 Nov): earthquakes with warnings (needs a seismicity rule from the plate
  stage), stellar flares and radiation at the surface (track O), dust storms, volcanic ash, ice, boulders below the
  sample spacing (a rock-size distribution from the regime). Each adds `Hazard` entries with its own `source`.
- **Path over the terrain**: telemetry has downrange but no ground track on tiles yet; the descent view (R2) needs
  the track as lat/lon points and the tiles along it.
- **Cover at the site** (ice, dunes, forest, built) is not yet a hazard.
- References written from memory to verify: Knacke 1992 (Parachute Recovery Systems Design Manual); stock lander
  numbers.
