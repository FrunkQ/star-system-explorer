# F5: the leftovers, as clouds that resolve

Status: done (18 Nov 2026). Physics track. Design: `SSE4-system-formation-design.md` section 3 (stage 7) and
section 8 (F5). Code: `crates/sse-formation/src/leftovers.rs`, `leftovers(seed, system, disc, upheaval)`.

## What was built
- **Clouds**: every F4 belt and reservoir becomes a `Cloud` at `<system>/cloud:N` with an orbital envelope, mass
  by element group, water by source band and a size law. Kinds: `asteroid-belt` (inside the innermost giant),
  `belt`, `outer-belt` (outside the outermost giant), `debris-belt` (F4 impacts), `scattered-disc` (1.5 to 30
  times its belt, e 0.2 to 0.8), `oort` (3,000 to 100,000 AU times M^(1/3), isotropic, thermal e; Duncan,
  Quinn and Tremaine 1987), `trojans` (on their giant's orbit, L4 share 0.6, Grav et al. 2011),
  `irregular-moons` (0.1 to 0.5 Hill radii, isotropic so about half retrograde; Jewitt and Haghighipour 2007),
  `ring`. Stirred belts are hot (e 0.15, i 8 degrees), quiet ones cold (0.05, 3 degrees).
- **Size law**: collisional, N(>D) proportional to D^-2.5 (Dohnanyi 1969); the k-th largest has mass m1 k^-1.2.
  The largest is capped at Pluto and Eris class, 2,400 km (ring particles at 10 m); a cloud too massive for one
  capped top body gets as many cap-size bodies as its mass needs (the primordial outer disc held 1,000 to 4,000
  Plutos, Nesvorny and Vokrouhlicky 2016), then the law. Weights are exact below 1,000 top bodies and
  Euler-Maclaurin above.
- **Resolve**: `Cloud::resolve(seed, k)` gives the k-th largest object at `<cloud>/obj:k`: size, mass, orbit (a,
  e, i, node, pericentre, mean anomaly) and Trojan swarm, from draws keyed by that address alone (stream
  `formation.leftovers.v1`). `Cloud::split(n)` splits the cloud's integer mass exactly into its n largest and the
  unresolved rest.
- **Regular moons**: each giant gives 1e-4 of its mass from its solids (Canup and Ward 2006; ledger entry
  `satellite-disc`, water bands kept) to 2 to 5 moons at `<system>/body:i/moon:j`, first at 4 to 8 planet radii,
  spacing ratio 1.5 to 2. A quarter of giants keep a massive ring, 1e-4 of the satellite disc at 1.2 to 2.3
  planet radii (Canup 2010).
- **Impact moons**: F4's oblique-impact moons, resolved at `<system>/body:i/impact_moon:j` at their birth
  distance of 3 to 6 planet radii (tides move them in F7); a moon whose world later merged away is addressed
  `<system>/lost_moon:j`.
- `Leftovers.worlds` are F4's worlds after the satellite discs; hand them to F6 with
  `violent::protoplanets_of(&leftovers.worlds, system)`.

## Checks (`leftovers_tests.rs`, 4 tests)
- Reservoirs conserve the budgets (300 systems): F4's worlds + belts + reservoirs + impact moons = worlds +
  clouds + moons, per element group, exactly; every cloud splits exactly at 0, 1, 10 and 1,000 objects, largest
  first; water lists sum to each holder's water; world ledgers still equal their worlds.
- A resolved object is the same whenever it is asked for (60 metal-rich systems): objects 1 to 20 asked forwards
  and backwards are identical, sorted by size, inside their envelope; regenerating the system gives the same
  object 7.
- Population (1,000 Sun-like systems): asteroid belts 179, outer belts 163, Oort clouds 116, Trojan swarms 108;
  228 giants, 50 ringed, 3.56 regular moons per giant, every satellite system 0.5 to 2 times 1e-4 of its giant.
- Golden `tests/golden/formation-leftovers.json` (the six golden systems, first three objects of every cloud),
  in the cross-device harness as `formation-leftovers`: Node and Chromium identical to native. The harness caught
  one real fault on the way: a draw key built from a `usize` index is 4 bytes on wasm32 and 8 natively; keys now
  use `u64` only. No existing golden changed.

## Calibrated or sketch values (named in the code)
SATELLITE_SHARE 1e-4, RING_CHANCE 0.25, RING_SHARE 1e-4, L4_SHARE 0.6, SIZE_CAP_KM 2,400, the envelopes above.

## Known limits
- Belt masses are what F3 and F4 leave; unstirred inner belts can hold more than the real asteroid belt (the
  Grand Tack style depletion is not modelled).
- Small rocky planets get no captured moons (Phobos and Deimos); moon systems are not yet disturbed by later
  events (F8 feeds the F4 cascade).
- Cloud objects are resolved on request only; nothing is stored per object.
