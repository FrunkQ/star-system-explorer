# U18: The chart's grid is its scale (UI track)

Status: done 22 Nov 2026. Alex asked whether the chart's background grid was scaled, "maybe 1 AU per square to be useful".

## What it does

- **Before:** the grid was 160 px squares with map references, drawn for looks and not to scale. A square could not have meant 1 AU, because the system chart is log-radial: from Mercury to Neptune, an AU near the star is many times wider on screen than an AU near the edge.
- **Now the grid is the scale.** It is drawn as rings round the star:
  - A bold dotted ring at each power of ten: 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1,000 AU, and so on. Each is labelled where it crosses above the star.
  - A faint dotted ring at 2 and 5 times each power when there is room (at least about 110 px between decades).
  - Round a planet (its moon frame), the rings are in kilometres: 100,000 km, 1,000,000 km.
  - The rings are dotted so that a solid line on the chart is always an orbit.
  - The legend says "Log scale: each bold dotted ring is ten times the last (1 AU, 10 AU…)".
- The lens's "grid" layer still switches them on and off, and "distances" switches their labels.
- `chart-grammar` gains `scaleRings(scale, r_out, unit_au)`. The square `SurveyGrid` stays for flat maps (hex maps, sector maps), where a square can mean a distance.

## Checks

- `packages/chart-grammar/test/scale.test.ts`: Mercury to Neptune gives rings at 1, 10 and 100 AU, with 0.5, 2 and 5 between. Every ring lies on the chart, in outward order. A cramped chart gives decades only. Kilometre rings round Jupiter's moons include 1,000,000 km.
- ui:check, ui:test and ui:e2e pass. No goldens change.
