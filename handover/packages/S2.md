# S2: tectonic regimes and the plate sketch (19 Nov 2026)

Surface design section 2.2 (and 2.1 via S0); research report section 3.2 stage 2. Needs P1, S1, T1 (done).
Physics track, overnight, at the coordinator's request.

## What was built
- `crates/sse-surface/src/plates.rs`, settings under `provides.other.surface.plates` in `packs/base-surface`.
- Grid: the cube grid, 64 × 64 cells a face (24,576 cells; the design asks 10,000 to 40,000). A direction finds its
  cell in O(1) through `face_of`.
- `history(settings, envelope, seed, epoch_gyr) -> PlateHistory`, stepped every 25 Myr through every mobile-lid
  span of the S0 timeline. Plates (8 to 16, keyed) grow from keyed points as a weighted Voronoi split; each turns
  about a keyed pole at a speed of about 4 cm/yr × spread × (heat flow / 0.039 W/m²). Continental nuclei cover
  22% at the start.
- Each step is semi-Lagrangian. Every fixed cell asks each plate whether the cell's material came from that plate
  (rotated back by the plate's turn).
  - No plate claims it: new ocean crust, dated (a ridge).
  - One plate: the crust moves on.
  - Several plates:
    - Continental crust never sinks. Otherwise one plate-level order decides which plate overrides: more
      continent first, then younger ocean. This keeps a boundary one-sided instead of speckled.
    - Ocean under a winner leaves a trench and an arc on the winner. Arcs thicken ocean crust (5 km a step)
      until it becomes continent at 25 km.
    - Continent meets continent: the crust thickens and a dated mountain-building episode is stamped. A front
      of 0.2% of all cells (49 cells) sutures the two plates while there are more than the minimum number of
      plates. A shorter front turns the smaller plate onto a new pole.
  - A majority filter (a cell disagreeing with three of its four neighbours joins them, crust kept) clears
    sampling strays.
- Keyed reorganisations are recorded as `PlateEvent`s: pole jumps, splits through a plate's centre, consumed
  plates, sutures and collisions. Their rate scales with the square root of heat flow, and splits are four
  times as likely below the minimum plate count.
- Plumes: 8 keyed plumes, fixed in the deep frame. Each stamps the cell above it each step; the stamp moves
  with the plate as an age-ordered chain. Rare flood basalts (2 per Gyr) cover a 0.12 rad disc.
- `boundaries`: for every cell, the nearest spreading, colliding and sliding boundary (breadth-first in cells,
  then km). Classes come from the relative plate velocity across the boundary.
- `cell_heights`:
  - Sea floor from crust age by GDH1 (Stein and Stein 1992). Continents at +400 m plus Airy thickening.
  - Mountain belts, arcs and plume volcanoes, each worn down with its own e-folding time. Relief saturates
    smoothly at 9 km × (9.81 / g). Trenches deepen recent subduction.
  - `sea_level` fits the world's water volume (Earth oceans × 1.335e18 m³).
- Snapshots of every cell's plate and continent flag every 4 steps (100 Myr) for the time ruler;
  `plates_at(t)`.
- Atlas: `Atlas::from_plates(..)` is the surveyed atlas.
  - Heights are bilinear over the plate grid (across face edges), shifted so sea level is 0, plus S1's
    band-limited detail octaves.
  - Material is granite or basalt from the crust. Cover is water below sea level. Surface age is crust age, or
    time since the last mountain building.
  - Its address is `shell:surface@<epoch>`. The sketch atlas is now `shell:sketch@<epoch>`, so the two never
    share tile addresses.
  - A world whose plates never moved keeps the sketch atlas. Its tolerance uses two plate cells as the
    smallest smooth feature.

## Checks (`cargo test -p sse-surface plates`)
- Earth fixture:
  - 176 steps, 3 to 20 plates at the end.
  - Continental area 15 to 45%. More than 100 ridge cells and more than 100 trench cells.
  - More than 50 cells on active arcs and more than 50 in mountain belts.
  - Collision, split and pole-jump events.
  - Two-peaked cell heights (Sarle above 5/9), with 15 to 45% land after sea level. Every height within ±12 km.
  - One run at 64 cells a face: 7 plates, 28% continent, 27% land, bimodality 0.68, heights −7.7 to +9.0 km.
- Mars, Mercury, Moon (stagnant lid): no steps, one plate, no boundaries, belts, arcs, snapshots or events.
- Crust ages:
  - Within one cell of a ridge, mean age is under 0.6 of the age 4 to 8 cells out.
  - Across spreading boundaries, the median age difference between the two sides is within 4 steps.
- Replay: the same call gives an identical history. A run to 2.1 Gyr is a prefix of the full run: same
  snapshots, and the last snapshot equals its cells. More than 10% of cells change continent flag between the
  first and last snapshot (drift).
- Surveyed atlas:
  - Six level-1 tiles give 10 to 50% land and two-peaked elevations.
  - Children downsample to parents within tolerance at levels 2 and 9.
  - Mars keeps the sketch atlas.
- Golden `tests/golden/surface-plates.json` is new. It covers Earth, Mars and the wet super-Earth on a
  16-a-face grid, with three surveyed tiles each, and is in the cross-device harness as `surface-plates`.
- `tests/golden/surface-atlas.json` was re-blessed on purpose: only the six atlas addresses changed
  (`surface@` to `sketch@`). The tile hashes are unchanged.

## Known limits
- Ocean crust lives too long. The mean is about 280 Myr against Earth's 60 Myr, because some large plates have
  little relative motion. Plate speeds and reorganisation rates are pack numbers to tune.
- Continental area is not conserved exactly. Collisions lose a row of cells and arcs add some back; the Earth run
  holds at 20 to 30%.
- Cells are 156 km on an Earth. Mountain belts, arcs and trenches are one or two cells wide. Finer ridges come
  from the detail octaves, not from the history.
- No rifting of continents into new ocean basins beyond what plate splits give. No back-arc basins yet.
- The stagnant lid and the other regimes keep the S0 sketch until S6. Plumes on a stagnant lid (Tharsis-like
  shields) are the sketch's three keyed shields for now.
- Sea level is fixed through time. S3 makes it follow ocean volume and basin shape.
- Cost: an Earth history at 64 a face takes about 1 s natively (release).
