# W9 part 1: climate supply to geology (21 Nov 2026)

Weather review (`handover/design/SSE4-weather-review.md`) section 8, package W9, under the processes-not-classes rule.
The finding it fixes: wear rained the climate's band rain straight into its rivers, froze glaciers below a fixed 263 K,
and allowed dunes only on dry worlds above 5 mbar, which forbade Pluto's dunes and the Sahara's alike.

W9 is landing in two parts so each lands clean. **Part 1** (this log) is the water and wind supply, glaciers from the
ice balance, dunes from saltation, and per-area climate labels. **Part 2** is the insolation history from T1's orbit
cycles and the glacial advances it drives (the review's obliquity-cycle test).

## What was built
- **`sse-climate::water`**: the water a place receives, at any latitude and height, from the one climate's seasons.
  - Temperature at the place is the band's season temperature less the lapse rate times the height.
  - Rain or snow: snow when the air is below freezing + 1.0 K (Jennings et al. 2018), with a 5 K scatter about the
    season's mean (Calov and Greve 2005), so each season's snow share is a normal-distribution fraction.
  - Melt: positive degree days with the same scatter (Calov and Greve 2005), 3.2 mm per K per day on snow and 8 on ice
    (Braithwaite 1995). The **ice balance** is snow less what melts; above zero a glacier grows.
  - Potential evaporation is the energy-limited flux the climate already uses, only while unfrozen.
  - Evaporation and runoff from the water reaching the ground (rain and melt) by Fu's Budyko curve, ω = 2.6 (Zhang et
    al. 2004). Every drop balances: rain + melt = evaporation + runoff.
  - The **area label**: the first `climate.areas` rule whose conditions hold. Ice cap from the ice balance (physics),
    then Kottek et al. 2006's Köppen-Geiger criteria for water worlds (frost, tundra, desert, steppe, rainforest,
    savanna, continental, temperate), and frozen, dry or wet for other liquids. Labels are pack data; nothing reads them.
- **`sse-climate::saltation`** (`sse.wind.saltation`, shared with geology's dunes and from W10 weather's dust):
  - fluid threshold of Shao and Lu 2000 as given by Kok et al. 2012 (A_N 0.111, cohesion 2.9e-4 N/m);
  - impact threshold 0.82 of it, held at its dense-air value in thin air (Kok 2010: Mars's is about Earth's);
  - law of the wall to 10 m with z0 = D/30; Weibull gusts, k = 2 (Kok 2010);
  - lofting by subliming ice where the air's main gas freezes onto the ground (Telfer et al. 2018);
  - transport share: P(above the impact threshold) wherever transport starts (lofted, or one ten-minute wind a year
    above the fluid threshold);
  - dune activity from Lancaster's mobility index M = W/(P/PE), inactive below 50, fully active above 200.
  - **No pressure cut.** The Moon (no wind) never moves sand; Pluto's 10 µbar can, given lofted grains.
- **Wear (S3)** now reads it, each epoch, at each cell's own height:
  - rivers carry the cell's **runoff**, not the band's rain (the sea's own rain no longer runs off);
  - a cell is **glaciated** where its ice balance is positive (Earth's tropical snow line now sits near 4.9 km, as
    observed, where the old 263 K cut gave about 6 km);
  - **dunes** where dune activity is at least 0.5 (`wear.dune_activity`) on flat, unglaciated land, wet world or dry;
  - `Wear` publishes `runoff_m_yr`, `snow_m_yr`, `ice_balance_m_yr`, `dune_activity` and `areas` per cell, for S9
    strata, S14b karst and the cover to read. `wear.ice_k`, `wear.dune_rain` and `wear.dune_pressure_bar` are gone.
- **Kernel** `survey_surface`: the climate summary gains `places`, one per band at the datum: area, label, because,
  rain, snow, runoff, ice balance, the share of time sand moves and dune activity.
- `sse-core::math` gains `erf`, `erfc` and `tgamma` (libm, pinned).
- Example `cargo run -p sse-climate --example water` prints every fixture's supply, saltation and label by latitude.

## Tests
- `sse-climate::water_tests`:
  - Earth's wettest band is within 10° of the equator, its driest in each hemisphere between 15° and 35° (labelled
    desert or rainless); ice caps at the poles, rainforest on the equator; every drop balances.
  - A mountain has its own snow line: no glacier at sea level at 45°, one at 4 km; snow changes smoothly with height.
  - Pluto: methane-ice sand; the fluid threshold needs over 100 m/s, the impact threshold under 10 m/s; with Telfer's
    6 m/s mean wind and lofted grains the sand moves and dunes are active; unlofted it never starts; the Moon never
    moves sand at any latitude.
  - Earth's Sahara band has active dunes with sand moving a few tens of percent of the time, threshold 6 to 12 m/s at
    10 m; the rainforest has none.
- `sse-surface::wear_tests::rivers_glaciers_and_dunes_read_the_one_climates_water_and_wind`: Earth's dunes lie in the
  dry belts; glaciers cover over 90 % of land poleward of 60° and only high mountains near the equator (above 4 km);
  ice caps, rainforest, temperate and desert areas all appear; Mars, dry now, has no runoff and has dunes.

## Goldens changed (once, on purpose)
`surface-wear.json`, `surface-cover.json` and the two `surface_surveyed` lines of `contract.jsonl`. Reason: rivers now
carry runoff rather than rain, glaciers follow the ice balance and dunes follow saltation (the accepted W9), and the
survey's climate summary gains `places`.

## Known limits (all in `docs/fudges.md`)
- One 5 K temperature scatter and Earth's degree-day factors on every world and ice.
- Potential evaporation is energy-limited only; the year is one budget with no seasonal storage.
- The impact threshold in thin air is held at Earth's value: Pluto's comes out near 3 m/s at 10 m against Telfer et
  al.'s 8.5 to 10 m/s, so our Pluto moves sand more easily.
- The 10 m wind and gusts are tuned shares of the climate's band and eddy winds. Our Mars winds rarely pass the fluid
  threshold away from the poles, so Mars's mid-latitude dunes are lofting-limited; Titan's mean wind (about 0.5 m/s)
  never reaches its 2 m/s threshold, so its dunes wait for W10's equinox storms.
- Lofting reads whether the main gas freezes out in the coldest season; our climate does not freeze its air out yet
  (and its nitrogen frost point at 1 Pa is 33.5 K, low against about 36.5 K), so Pluto's own lofting reads about 0.01
  until W8's exchange and S17's ground ice.
- No sand supply: Venus gets dunes everywhere its gentle winds allow, while Magellan saw few.
- The grain is chosen by temperature (methane ice, water ice or rock, 250 µm) until geology publishes its loose grains.
- Cover (S7) still places its land ice by temperature; it can switch to `Wear.ice_balance_m_yr` (proven-core thread).
- Iversen and White 1982, which the review named, is not used: its coefficients could not be read, and Kok et al. give
  Shao and Lu's form whole.
