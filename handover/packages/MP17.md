# MP17: many phases per substance, and `phase_at`

Matter pack design (`design/SSE4-matter-pack-design.md`) section 4.3A, package MP17. It was added on Alex's word of 22 Nov 00:50 ("a LOT more ice types all overlaid on Water ... the ice that created Neptune's mag field should be calculable, and worked out from that table"). Built 22 Nov 2026 by the kernel thread. Questions 22 to 25 are still open, so it is built to the recommendations.

## What it does
`crates/sse-reference/src/phases.rs` reads a substance's phase list alongside its familiar values. Old readers see the same `phases` object as before: the triple point, the vapour curve and the densities stay where they were.

- **Agreed with the data sheet thread, which builds MP18 (water's ices).** These keys all sit inside the existing `phases` object:
  - `phases.forms`: `{id: "<substance>.<phase>", name{physics,plain,story}, kind, stability, evidence{grade, p_pa, t_k, src}, props{...}}`. `kind` is a label that nothing reads; blocks read `props`.
  - `phases.boundaries`: `{between: [a, b], curve, latent_heat?, dv?, evidence, src}`. The curve forms the checks can draw are:
    - `points`: `[[p_pa, t_k], ...]`;
    - `vapour`: the row's own `vapour_pressure` fit;
    - `iapws_melt`: `{tn, pn, log, terms: [[a, b], ...], valid_k}`, the IAPWS R14-08 form π = 1 + Σ a(1 − θ^b), or ln π = Σ a(1 − θ^b) when `log` is set.

    Other forms load but are not drawn into the checks.
  - `phases.meets`: triple points `{phases: [a, b, c], t_k, p_pa, src}`, each written once.
  - `phases.table`: `{log10_p_pa: {lo, hi, n}, t_k or log10_t_k: {lo, hi, n}, phases: [ids], cells or rle}`, built at pack-build time by `scripts/build-base-reference.py`.
    - `cells` is one base-36 string, row-major with pressure outer.
    - `rle` is the same grid run-length encoded: rows (low pressure first) split by "/", and runs (low temperature first) split by spaces. Each run is the phase digit followed by its length in base 36.
    - The real water map uses `rle` and a log temperature axis from 10 K to 10,000 K. Its 2201 × 1501 nodes are about 7,500 runs.
    - The reader keeps the runs, decoded once at load, and a lookup is a binary search within one row.

  The familiar solid, liquid and vapour of water are `h2o.ice_ih`, `h2o.liquid` and `h2o.vapour`.
- **Load checks:**
  - Form ids carry the substance's prefix and are unique.
  - Kind, stability and evidence grade must each be one of their known values. The grades are measured, computed, extrapolated and convention; convention is for lines where nothing physical changes, such as supercritical fluid against liquid and vapour.
  - A computed phase or boundary must name a source with a DOI or URL, so the paper that computed it is a link wherever it is shown (Alex, 22 Nov 01:08).
  - Every evidence and property `src` must name a source.
  - Boundaries and triple points may name only listed forms, and no triple point is written twice.
  - The solid-liquid-vapour triple point must agree with `triple_point_k` and `triple_point_pa`.
  - Every drawable boundary between two phases of a triple point must pass through that point.
  - The table's size, its base-36 cells and its ids are checked, and it may hold no metastable phase.
  - Along each drawable boundary, the table must show that boundary's own two phases either side, a grid step and a half across it in temperature or in pressure, for at least 80% of the sampled points. Checking both directions lets a boundary that is nearly flat in one axis, such as V against VI, be crossed in the other. A boundary with no sample inside the table, such as XI against vapour below 1e-10 Pa, is not checked.
- **`Reference::phase_at(substance, p_pa, t_k)`** reads the nearest grid node of the table, so a query is one lookup and gives the same answer on every device. It returns the phase id, its row, its evidence grade (which the magnetic fields track needs) and the source row with its DOI or URL. Outside the table, or for a substance without one, it gives the familiar reading:
  - **Below the triple point:** solid or vapour, split by the sublimation line.
  - **Above it:** liquid or vapour, split by Clausius-Clapeyron from the triple point with the row's latent heat of vaporisation. For water that puts boiling near 373 K at one atmosphere.

  Each answer says which reading it used (`from: "table"` or `"familiar"`).
- **Kernel op `phase_at {substance, p_pa, t_k}`** (light, read-only, any seat) replies with `matter`, whose `value` holds the answer.

## Verified by
- `a_water_map_loads_and_answers` uses a four-phase toy map (Ih, liquid, vapour, VII) whose table is classified from the same lines as its boundaries. It checks:
  - ice at 250 K and liquid at 300 K, both at one atmosphere;
  - vapour at 1 kPa and 300 K, and at 10 Pa and 240 K;
  - ice at 10 Pa and 200 K;
  - liquid at 300 MPa and 260 K, above Ih's melting line;
  - ice VII at 10 GPa;
  - the familiar reading outside the table.
- `a_run_length_table_reads_as_the_plain_one` and `a_log_temperature_axis_reads`.
- `the_iapws_melting_line_reads_its_published_points`: Ih melts at 208.566 MPa at 251.165 K (IAPWS R14-08), within 10⁻⁴, and the line starts at the triple point.
- `each_broken_rule_is_named`: twelve broken rows, each refused with its own message.
- `a_row_with_one_phase_still_loads_and_answers`.
- `the_base_sheet_answers_from_the_familiar_three`: the real sheet's water, with its phase lists and table taken away, reads as solid, liquid or vapour, and boils between 365 and 380 K at one atmosphere.
- `water_on_the_sheet_reads_at_published_points` reads the sheet on main, which holds water's table since MP18 (PR 132) merged. All checks pass on it, and the test gives:
  - Ih at 250 K and 1 bar;
  - liquid at 300 K and vapour at 400 K, both at 1 bar;
  - vapour at 1e-7 Pa and 150 K, Ih at 1 Pa;
  - III at 250 MPa and 250 K, II at 300 MPa and 230 K;
  - V at 500 MPa and 250 K;
  - VI at 1 GPa and 270 K, liquid at 330 K;
  - VII at 10 GPa;
  - a conducting form at 200 GPa and 4,000 K, with a linked source.

  Loading the whole sheet with its 4401 × 1501 table took well under a second.
- `phase_at_answers_through_the_kernel` (sse-kernel): liquid from the table, graded measured, at 1 atm and 300 K; Ih at 250 K; VI at 1 GPa and 270 K.

## Not done
- **A per-cell certainty.** phase_at gives the form's grade. A cell near a computed boundary could also give that boundary's grade, the lower of the two, which is what the data sheet thread suggests for ice XVIII (fcc lattice measured by Millot et al. 2019, edges computed).
- **MP17a**, the Look up block `sse.reference.lookup` (design section 3A).
- **A mixture's layer** asking for its main substance.
- **The interior walk** that keeps a world's phase profile for the cutaway and the dynamo test.
