# O10 (first slice): stellar activity, cycles, flares and mass ejections

Design: `SSE4-nebulae-and-light-design.md` section 4A; stage O10 in section 8. Atoms `sse.activity.*` in
`sse-atoms::activity`; crate `sse-activity`; kernel op `read_activity`.

## What was built
- Six atoms (editable blocks): convective turnover time from mass (Wright et al. 2011), Rossby number,
  X-ray fraction with saturation (log −3.13 below Ro 0.13, slope −2.70 above), cycle period on the inactive and
  active branches (Mittag et al. 2023), flare rate above an energy (power law α = 2, normalised to Vasilyev et al.
  2024's 8.63×10⁻³ per year above 10³⁴ erg for a star with the Sun's X-rays).
- `sse_activity::activity(seed, star, settings)`, keyed on conditions, never on a kind:
  - compact remnant (radius under 0.05 R☉): no activity of this kind;
  - 10 000 K or hotter: hot-wind X-rays at 10⁻⁷ L_bol (Pallavicini et al. 1981), no flares;
  - 6900 to 10 000 K: no corona, no spots, no flares;
  - cooler: turnover time, Rossby number, X-rays, spots, a cycle (none when saturated) and flares.
  - Spin: given, or held to a companion's orbit below 10 days (tides), or from age (Skumanich).
- `moment(activity, t)`: cycle phase, X-rays, spots and flare rates at a moment.
- `flares(seed, activity, from, to, min_energy, targets)`: a Poisson process thinned by the cycle, in fixed time
  bins per energy decade with draws keyed on (star, decade, bin). Any window and any lowest energy give the same
  flares where they overlap. Each flare has energy, rise, decay, peak power and a light description (10 000 K flash
  plus soft X-rays). Larger flares carry an ejection (speed, mass, direction, cone) that hits or misses each target
  at the place the target will be when it arrives, with the warning time light gives. Over 2000 flares, the
  smallest are summarised (count and energy).
- Kernel op `read_activity { stars: [Star + targets], seed?, at_s?, window? { from_s, to_s, min_energy_j } }` →
  `activity_read { stars: [{ activity, moment, flares? }] }`; error `bad_activity`.
- The X-ray power is what `sse-light` takes as `corona_xray_w` (B1 can wire it).

## Evidence (`cargo test -p sse-activity`, 8 tests, golden `tests/golden/activity.json`; `sse-atoms` 1 new test)
- Sun: turnover 14.45 days, Rossby 1.81, log(L_X/L_bol) = −6.21 against −6.24 observed; X-rays 2.4×10²⁰ W
  (inside the 1 to 5 ×10²⁰ W soft X-ray range over a cycle); about one 10³² erg flare a year; superflares
  8.6×10⁻³ per year; spot coverage 0.3 %; spin from age alone gives 26.09 days.
- Cycle: the inactive branch gives the Sun 6.1 years, not 11. The source says so too (the Sun sits between the
  branches); an authored cycle wins, so the Sun pack should author 11 years.
- EV Lacertae (0.32 M☉, 4.38-day spin): saturated at 10⁻³·¹³, no clean cycle, over 5 big flares a year.
- Young Sun from age alone: saturated at 20 million years, not by 100 million.
- Vega-like 9600 K: no corona; 12 100 K: hot-wind X-rays; white dwarf: compact; an 8-billion-year Sun-like star
  with a 2-day companion stays active.
- Power law: 10 years of EV Lac flares give about ten times as many per decade below; the total matches the
  expected count to 8 %; the flare's published light totals peak × 1.1.
- Keying: a long window and a short one, or a low and a high energy floor, list identical flares where they overlap.
- Ejections over 100 years of the Sun: some hit Earth, more miss; every hit lies inside the cone at arrival; the
  warning is 0.3 to 5 days.

## Stand-ins (to add to docs/fudges.md)
- Spin from age: Skumanich P ∝ √t scaled by turnover time from the Sun, never faster than a 1-day birth spin.
  Real fully convective red dwarfs stay fast for longer; the life line's spin should replace this (F7).
- Tidal locking assumed below a 10-day companion orbit.
- Hot-star, no-corona and compact cut-offs at 10 000 K, 6900 K and 0.05 R☉.
- Regime labels: active above 10⁻⁴, moderate above 3×10⁻⁷.
- Spot coverage ∝ √(L_X/L_bol) from the Sun's 0.3 %, capped at 50 %.
- Cycle: sine of amplitude 0.7, branch chosen at L_X/L_bol = 10⁻⁵; no grand minima.
- Flares: durations ∝ E^0.39 from 600 s at 10²⁵ J, rise a tenth of decay; light a 10 000 K black body plus 10 % soft
  X-rays; no radio burst; largest flare 10²⁹ J.
- Ejections: share 20 % at 10²³ J rising to all at 10²⁵ J; speed 400 km/s × (E/10²³ J)^0.2, at most 4000 km/s;
  mass 10¹² kg × (E/10²³ J)^0.6; cone half-width 30°, leaving within 30° of the equator; targets on circular orbits
  in the star's equator.
- Giants are treated with the main-sequence turnover fit (flagged when the mass is outside its range).
