# T1: epoch stepping, the ageing star, secular orbits, detailed windows

Status: done (18 Nov 2026). Kernel track, branch `claude/project-thread-dzkz4p`. Design: handoff section 6.3; build
plan row T1 (P1, K3).

## What was built
New crate `sse-time` (path dependencies only; root `Cargo.toml` untouched).
- `kepler.rs`: elements to state and back (au, yr, solar masses, G = 4 pi^2); Kepler's equation by Newton from
  Danby's start, a fixed 20 iterations.
- `nbody.rs`: the detailed-window integrator, **written for SSE4 from the published methods** (leapfrog
  drift-kick-drift, Yoshida 1990's fourth-order triple jump, w1 = 1/(2 - 2^(1/3)), w0 = -2^(1/3) w1). Not
  translated from REBOUND or any other code; REBOUND was not consulted. Fixed step, pairwise forces in a fixed order,
  barycentric frame, Jacobi elements in and out (Murray and Dermott 1999 section 9.5). The star's mass can follow a
  function of time (winds).
- `secular.rs`: first-order Laplace-Lagrange theory (Murray and Dermott 1999 chapter 7): A and B matrices with
  Laplace coefficients (1024-point trapezoid, exact for periodic integrands), symmetrised with
  w = sqrt(m sqrt(a)), eigenmodes by a fixed 60-sweep Jacobi rotation; advancing is a rotation of each mode.
- `star.rs`: the ageing star. **F0's life line is reused, not duplicated.** Added on top, in this crate:
  - a giant-branch **core clock** for stars with a degenerate helium core (F0's tip at 2500 L_sun, white dwarf
    remnant): core mass-luminosity L = 2.3e5 Mc^6 (Boothroyd and Sackmann 1988) and dMc/dt proportional to L, so
    Mc^-5 falls linearly with age between F0's base and tip luminosities. The life line is read at the age where
    it has that luminosity; the radius comes from HPT00's giant-branch law at the current, wind-reduced mass (as
    HPT00 does). This keeps F0's ages (t_BGB, t_end) and fixes the shape: most of the giant branch is faint and
    the climb to the tip is fast. Without it, Reimers winds on F0's geometric luminosity strip the Sun to its core
    before the tip.
  - Reimers 1975 winds, eta 0.5, fixed steps even in log luminosity; the rest of the envelope goes at the
    planetary nebula.
  - colour names by effective temperature (pack data) and blackbody sRGB (Helland's fit).
- `epoch.rs`: the epoch stepper. Steps: 250 Myr on the main sequence, 8 subgiant steps, 80 giant steps even in log
  L plus the tip itself, log steps for white dwarf cooling. Each step: star from the life line and wind table;
  orbits widen as a M = constant (Jeans 1924); giant tides drag planets in (Zahn 1977 equilibrium tide in the form
  of Villaver and Livio 2009 eqs. 7 and 8, f = (P / 2 tau)^2 capped at 1, Verbunt and Phinney 1995; corrected 19 Nov, it was inverted), in 8 fixed substeps;
  Laplace-Lagrange modes rebuilt for the step's star and orbits; engulfment at a(1 - e) <= R_star; named
  transitions dated by fixed 60-step bisection.
  - Detailed windows open at phase transitions and when two neighbours are closer than 2 sqrt(3) mutual Hill radii
    (Gladman 1993). A window runs 200 yr at 120 fourth-order steps per innermost orbit and reports energy error,
    steps and the largest change of a. Osculating elements carry short-period terms (Saturn's a swings by a few
    parts in a thousand), so a window rewrites the mean orbits only when some a changed by more than 2 percent.
  - Events carry a plain "why", the numbers behind it, a fidelity level (standard or sketch) and
    `plausible_future` when beyond the predictability horizon (60 Myr; Laskar 1989, 1994).
  - Progress is counted in deterministic work units (epoch steps plus window steps); `run_with` takes a callback
    that sees the work done and can cancel.
- Pack `packs/base-time` (`provides.other.time`): every setting above, each with its source.
- Fixture `tests/fixtures/time/solar-system.json`: the Sun (1 solar mass, Z 0.02, 4.57 Gyr), the eight planets at
  J2000 mean elements (Standish 1992), the asteroid belt.

## Checks (`cargo test -p sse-time`, the T1 part: 7 tests)
- Energy drift, Sun-Jupiter-Saturn, 20 000 yr: fourth order at dt 0.1 yr, worst relative error 5.1e-7; second
  order at 0.05 yr, 1.5e-5. No secular growth (the second half no worse than 3 times the first).
- Two-body against Kepler, e = 0.3: errors fall 16.0 times when the step halves (fourth order) and 3.9 times
  (second order). At 1000 steps a year over 150 yr: da 2.5e-10, de 3.8e-10, mean anomaly 1.5e-7 rad.
- Adiabatic invariant: a star losing half its mass over 3000 orbits; a M = 1.00000, e stays below 2e-4.
- Laplace-Lagrange against the integrator, error reported:
  - a well-separated pair (period ratio 8), half a secular period (28 kyr): e swings by 0.028, theory within
    0.0043 (16 percent of the swing), i within 0.06 degrees.
  - Jupiter and Saturn, 20 kyr: e swings by 0.042, theory within 0.022, i within 0.05 degrees. Loose because
    first-order theory leaves out the near 5:2 (the Great Inequality): its g6 is 22.3 arcsec/yr against 27.8 with
    those terms. Bound 0.03.
- Solar System frequencies, all eight planets: g = 0.63, 2.70, 3.71, 5.45, 7.33, 17.27, 17.96, 22.28 and
  s = -25.73, -18.67, -17.60, -6.56, -5.19, -2.90, -0.68, 0 arcsec/yr. All within 2 percent of Brouwer and van
  Woerkom 1950 except g5 and g6, which match first-order theory (Murray and Dermott 3.7 and 22.3).
- The ageing Sun: brightens 5 to 15 percent per Gyr from now; red giant tip at 2500 L_sun, 182 R_sun (0.85 au),
  0.645 solar masses, core 0.471 (Schroeder and Smith 2008: 256 R_sun, 0.67); half way through the giant branch by
  age it is still under 10 L_sun; mass never rises or falls below the core.
- Ageing the Solar System to 10 Gyr past the nebula (also T2's scenario): every event explained and in date order,
  oceans lost at 6.12 Gyr (1.5 Gyr from now), Mercury engulfed at 12.73 Gyr, Earth and the giants survive on orbits
  widened 1.76 times, the white dwarf cools through five colours, windows hold energy to better than 1e-5 and
  rewrite nothing, two runs are identical.

## Known limits
- The giant phase is F0's sketch with the core clock on top: no horizontal branch or asymptotic giant branch yet
  (the red giant tip stands in for both tips), so the Sun's largest radius is 0.85 au where detailed models give
  1.0 to 1.2 au. Since the tide factor was corrected (19 Nov, CITE.md; it was inverted) Venus is engulfed and Earth survives
  pulled in about 3 percent from pure widening; Schroeder and Smith 2008 find Venus and probably Earth engulfed. The event text and the level ("sketch") say so. **For the physics track (F7):** when
  F7 brings giant tables, `AgeingStar::star_with` and `core_at` are the two places to read them; the core clock
  then goes. Coordinate through the handover.
- Tides: one equilibrium-tide formula with an envelope of mass minus core and a convective radius equal to the
  star's; no dynamical tides, no planet spin.
- Secular theory is first order (no mean-motion resonance terms); windows are short checks, not a long N-body run.
- Planets that are not engulfed but cross inside the star's tidal reach are not torn apart (no Roche check yet).
- Pollution is a sketch: dated 100 Myr after the nebula when a giant planet lies outside a debris belt.
