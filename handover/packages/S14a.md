# S14a: lava tubes, part 1 (envelopes)

Design: `handover/design/SSE4-rock3-comparison.md` section 5.5 (accepted 21 Nov; S14a is first among the new
features, and Alex's favourite). Built under the processes-not-classes rule: every number is a process
strength or a cited scaling.

## What it does
`sse-surface/src/tubes.rs` builds an envelope for every lava source in the world. Passages are not placed
yet. The lava sources are:
- S4 shield volcanoes, on their flanks;
- S4 lava plains, on the gentle ramps at their margins;
- on plate worlds, hot-spot volcanoes. These are the S2 plume cells that erupted in the last 10 Myr and stand
  above the sea now. Older plume tracks have sunk as seamounts, whose lava erupted under water as pillows,
  not tubes. Arc volcanoes are left out because their viscous lava makes no tubes.

Each `TubeField` holds:
- **forming**, 0 to 1. This is a log-normal curve in slope, best near 2°, falling to e^(−1/2) at a factor of
  2.5 either side (Greeley 1971; Calvari and Pinkerton 1998). Shield flanks use S4's shield aspect, which
  gives about 4.8°.
- **width_m**, between Earth's typical and largest widths (10 and 30 m; Sauro et al. 2020), scaled by 1/g,
  because a roof spans wider as gravity falls (Blair et al. 2017). The Moon allows about 6 times Earth's
  width and Mars about 2.6 times. The roof is a quarter of the width.
- **length_km** of passage, which is 0.05 km per km² × forming × the field's area × the share not buried by
  later resurfacing (`CraterField::renewal_kept`).
- **roof_kept**, 0 to 1, from two losses:
  - Impacts breach the roof wherever a crater is at least 5 roof thicknesses wide. This is the saturation
    law, the same one that thins old craters.
  - Rain wears the roof down at 20 m per Myr per metre of rain a year from the one climate, over the
    world's wet span.
- **stage**, read off roof_kept: intact roof (0.9 and above), skylights, pit chains (0.1 to 0.5), and a
  sinuous rille once the roof is gone.
- **skylights**, a count from the length of roof lost.
- **speculative**, set for cryolava on an ice world.
- **cause**: the why-answer, with the numbers and citations.

`Survey.tubes` carries the envelopes at both the Orbit and Survey levels; they are cheap. The kernel's
`survey_surface` returns them as `tubes`.

## Not in part 1 (see part 2 below for what has landed)
- The passage graph on approach: a keyed draw from the envelope that follows the downhill path from the
  vent over the atlas heights. It gives nodes, segments, widths, heights, depth, and entrances where
  skylights and rilles meet the surface. These are kernel facts, identical on every device and nameable.
- The cave survey map (plan and profile) in the shell, and a void band in the Cutaway.
- Io and Venus get no tubes because the S4 volcanism stage stops making new plains once its cover cap is
  reached, while renewal buries the old ones. Young flows on resurfaced worlds need S4 to keep erupting
  (or the renewal itself to be the lava that holds tubes).
- Lunar maria come out about 4.5 Gyr old, which is S4's volcanism timing; real maria are 3 to 3.9 Gyr old.
  Younger maria would keep more roofs.
- The `tubes` in survey replies are not yet read by the shell. The Body view's why-panel and the
  sifter's "tubes wider than 300 m" query need a shell package.

# Part 2: passages on approach (Site level)

`sse-surface/src/caves.rs`, kernel op `survey_caves` (`crates/sse-kernel/src/caves.rs`).

A cave survey at a site places the lava-tube passages within `radius_km` (default 5 km, at most the pack's
`caves.window_max_km`, 25 km) from the tube fields of part 1. Nothing is placed for a world that is only
glanced at: the passages exist only when a site asks.

## How
- **One world from any site.** Each tube is keyed by its field and a fixed 10 km grid cell in the field's own
  (gnomonic) frame, never by the window, so the same tube found from two sites is identical, node for node.
  Draws come from stream `surface.caves.v1` under the world's own seed (`Survey.seed`, new).
- **How many.** Each cell starts a Poisson number of tubes whose mean makes the field's passage per km² match
  the envelope's `length_km` over its area. Tube length is log-uniform from 0.5 to 15 km.
- **Path.** Lava runs downhill: from the start, the steepest of eight headings; then each step tries five
  headings within 40° (with a small keyed jitter) and takes the lowest ground on the world's one height map.
  At most 40 steps of 50 to 400 m; a tube stops past 1.2 field radii.
- **Size.** A tube's width scatters about the field's (log-normal, σ 0.25) and never passes the widest roof
  that holds (Blair et al. 2017); segments vary ±20% under the same cap. Passage height is 0.6 of the
  width; the floor lies a roof plus a passage below the ground.
- **Roof.** Each segment's roof stands or has fallen, as a two-state chain whose fallen share is the
  envelope's lost roof (1 − roof_kept). Fallen runs average 2 segments; where most roof is gone, standing
  runs shrink to one segment and the fallen runs lengthen. A fallen run of one segment is a skylight, two to
  four a pit chain, more an open rille. Every place a roof ends is an entrance.
- **Height reads.** `Atlas::heights(level)` (new) keeps each tile's gathered features and landforms for
  many reads close together, the same sum as `height_at`. A Moon survey of 15 km took 12 s with plain
  `height_at` and about 80 ms with the reader. Paths read the level whose spacing matches the step
  (level 5, 334 m, on the Moon).

## Reply
`caves_surveyed { caves: {version, lat_deg, lon_deg, radius_km, level, spacing_m, fields, passages, why} }`.
Each passage: id (`<field>:<i>:<j>:<n>`), field, length_km, width_m, roof_m, depth_m, nodes (lat, lon,
x_m and y_m east and north of the site for the survey map, ground_m, floor_m, entrance), segments (from,
to, length_m, width_m, height_m, roofed, open), entrances, speculative, cause. `why` says what was found or
why nothing was (no lava field reaches the site, or none of its passages run near it).

## Checks
- `caves_tests::passages_are_one_world_from_any_site`: a repeat survey is identical, and a tube seen from
  a window 5 km away is the same tube.
- `caves_tests::passages_keep_to_their_envelope`: widths under the cap, height 0.6 of width, the floor below
  the ground, an entrance exactly where a roof ends, and the fallen share within 0.15 of the envelope's lost
  roof over the Moon's first six fields.
- `caves_tests::no_lava_no_tubes`: Europa has none and says why.
- Contract: three `survey_caves` cases (a Moon field with most roof standing, an old Moon field now a rille,
  and Jupiter, which has no ground). The contract golden is re-blessed on purpose for them.

## Fudges (registered in docs/fudges.md)
- Passage height 0.6 of width, width scatter, length range, fallen-run length, the keying cell.
- Steepest descent at a coarse atlas level: real tubes also follow valleys finer than the level read.

## Part 3: roofs holed only where a bowl cuts deeper than the roof (24 Nov)

The TRUE6 thread found that the catalogued Moon's tube roofs were all breached. The cause: a roof's survival was read from the crater saturation law (`erasure_survival` at a size five times the roof). That law asks whether ground of that size has been erased at all, and on the old maria the answer is almost always yes.

- **`Craters::cut_through_kept(depth_m, t0, t1)`** (impacts.rs) is new. It takes craters by size bin from the production function, between the roof's date and now. Each fresh bowl's depth comes from `crater_shape` (Pike 1977), and the bowl is treated as a paraboloid, so it cuts below the roof only over 1 − roof/depth of its floor. The roof share left is e^(−A), where A is the area cut. This does not saturate: a hole, once made, stays a hole.
- `tubes.rs` uses it in place of `erasure_survival`.
- Result: the fixture Moon now runs from rilles on the oldest maria through pit chains and skylights to intact roofs on the younger ones. Earth's young tubes stay intact. Mars keeps its wear-driven spread.
- Tests:
  - `tube_roofs_scale_with_gravity_and_wear_with_time` now also asks for an intact Moon roof (Haruyama et al. 2009, the Marius Hills hole);
  - `sol_tests::the_moons_younger_maria_keep_roofed_tubes` checks the catalogued Moon.
- Golden: re-blessed on purpose. Two survey lines in contract.jsonl carry tubes, and their `roof_kept` and stage change with the new breach law.
