# S1: surface atlas (19 Nov 2026)

Surface design section 1 ("Tiles", "The fact floor", "Survey and disclosure"); research report section 3.1 and
3.4. Needs K2 and K9 (done). Physics track, overnight, at the coordinator's request.

## What was built
- `crates/sse-surface/src/atlas.rs`, settings under `provides.other.surface.atlas` in `packs/base-surface`.
- Addressing: `TileKey { face, level, x, y }`; six cube faces, each 2^level × 2^level tiles of `tile_samples`
  (256) square. Address `<body>/shell:surface@<epoch Myr>/tile:<face>.<level>.<x>.<y>`: dots, not the design's
  slashes, because an address key cannot hold a slash.
- Geometry: cube-sphere with the tangent warp (`direction`, `face_of`), cell-centred samples so a child's 2 × 2
  samples sit inside the parent's cell. Level 1 is the design's "level 0 global survey" (6 faces of 512 × 512,
  about 20 km a sample on an Earth); the code's level 0 is one 256 tile per face.
- Height: the S0 `SketchModel` (now a function at any direction, constants fixed once per world on a 32 × 64
  reference grid) plus keyed 3D value-noise detail octaves (longest 0.02 rad, Hurst 0.8, 8% of the sketch relief).
  **Band-limited**: a level carries an octave only when its wavelength is at least 4 sample spacings, so a
  level's samples are fixed and a child adds only the octaves its finer spacing can hold.
- Layers per sample: elevation (decimetres, integer), material (`MATERIALS`), cover (`COVERS`), slope (tenths
  of a degree, from a one-sample border built with the tile), surface age (Myr since the current regime began;
  provisional until S2/S4), albedo (thousandths), tags (empty until S4/S7). `pending` lists what later stages
  fill. `hash` covers every layer.
- Disclosure: unexamined gives no tiles (the globe uses the sketch's colour); scanned gives level 0; surveyed
  down to the 100 m fact floor; landed down to the 10 m site floor inside declared `Site`s (tile centre within
  the site radius plus 3/4 of a tile). Finer requests are refused as `BelowFactFloor` (decoration, GPU).
- `TileCache`: bounded, evicts by a use counter (never a clock), re-checks disclosure on every request.
- Epochs: `Atlas::new(.., epoch_gyr, ..)` takes the regime in force at that date from the S0 timeline, so a young
  Earth's atlas is a magma ocean and today's has continents; each epoch has its own address.

## Checks (`cargo test -p sse-surface atlas`)
- Any tile builds alone: a fresh atlas building tiles in the reverse order gives identical tiles.
- Children downsample to parents within `Atlas::tolerance_m` (stated formula: added octaves' amplitude, plus each
  carried octave's and the sketch's curvature over one parent cell, plus rounding) for Earth, Mars, Europa, Io and
  Vesta at levels 0 to 15. Measured at 256 samples: Earth level 8 to 9 worst 4.1 m against 5.1 m stated.
- Faces round-trip through `face_of`; spacing is as stated.
- Disclosure and floors as above, including a landing site sharpening only its own tiles.
- Level 0 over six faces: Earth's land fraction 15 to 50%; Jupiter flat, all cloud.
- The cache never changes a tile; eviction and disclosure work.
- Golden `tests/golden/surface-atlas.json` (new; six worlds, eight tiles each at 32 samples, levels 0, 6 and 13),
  in the cross-device harness as `surface-atlas`: identical in Node and Chromium. No existing golden changed
  (the S0 sketch refactor leaves `surface.json` byte-identical).
- Cost: a 256 × 256 tile builds in about 0.1 s natively in one thread (six level-1 tiles 0.7 s with test start-up).

## Known limits
- The height source is the S0 sketch plus noise: no plates, rivers, craters or named features yet (S2 to S4).
- Slope near a face edge uses samples just over the edge from the same face's extended coordinates; neighbours on
  the next face agree only approximately until S2 (the function is the same; the sample positions differ).
- Fact-floor numbers (100 m, 10 m) and detail amplitude are the design's estimates, set in the pack.
- No kernel op yet; the U2 body view or terrain chart is the first consumer. Tiles are plain serde data, ready
  to send peer to peer.
