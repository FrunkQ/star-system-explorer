# W8 part 1: one species table (21 Nov 2026)

Weather review (`handover/design/SSE4-weather-review.md`) package W8, section 4.3, folded into the matter pack as MP3
(`handover/design/SSE4-matter-pack-design.md` sections 6.2 and 10). W8 lands in two parts. **Part 1** (this log)
is the species table. **Part 2** is `sse.volatile.exchange`: ground ice and air, Mars's pressure cycle, and Pluto's
and Triton's ices.

## What was built
- **Ice curves on the master data sheet** (`scripts/build-base-reference.py`, then `packs/base-reference`). Each
  curve is Fray and Schmitt 2009's recommended fit, ln P[bar] = A0 + Σ A_i/T^i, with the coefficients read from
  Lisse et al. 2021's reproduction of the table (SOM Table 2). The curves:
  - N2: β above 35.61 K, α below;
  - CO: β above 61.55 K, α below;
  - CO2: two ranges, split at 194.7 K;
  - CH4, NH3 and Ar;
  - water, by Feistel and Wagner 2007 in Fray and Schmitt's form.

  I worked out which fit covers which range, and that the unit is bar, by passing each fit through its own triple
  point: all are within 3 %. New sheet values:
  - triple points for CO and Ar;
  - solid densities for N2, CO, Ar and CH4 (from memory, marked unverified);
  - condensed colours for CH4, NH3, N2, CO and Ar, authored and moved from the climate pack.
- **Reader** (`sse-reference`):
  - the `feistel-wagner-2007` fit;
  - `sublimation_pa`, which picks `vapour_pressure` or `vapour_pressure_low` by range and goes on below the lowest
    range as Clausius-Clapeyron;
  - `sublimation_latent_j_mol`, read off the curve's slope.

  Two new load checks: a low piece must meet the main one, and a typed sublimation heat must agree with the slope
  where it was measured (CO2 and NH3 pass).
- **`sse.volatile.species`** (`sse_climate::settings::volatile_species`): the climate's substances are now every
  sheet row with a triple point and a vapour curve. Molar masses come from the formula; the latent heat is the
  sheet's, or the slope's. The climate pack's own `substances` list is retired, and a pack that still carries one
  is refused.
- `ebm::p_sat` reads the solid's curve below the triple point. `ebm::frost_k` is its exact inverse (60 bisection
  steps), and `strengths::condense_k` uses it. Ground frost, cloud and the collapse test therefore read one curve.
  CO and Ar now condense where their partial pressure allows.

## Tests
- `sse-reference::the_shipped_ice_curves_give_known_frost_points`:
  - water ice gives 0.1626 Pa at 200 K;
  - Mars's CO2 freezes near 148 K at 610 Pa;
  - Pluto's nitrogen freezes near 37 K at 1 Pa;
  - the slopes give CO2's 25 kJ/mol and N2's 6 to 7.6 kJ/mol.
- `sse-climate::species_tests`:
  - the climate reads its seven volatiles off the sheet and refuses a pack's own table;
  - `p_sat(frost_k(p)) = p` for every species from 10⁻⁶ to 0.3 of its triple pressure;
  - Pluto's N2 frost point at 1 Pa is 36 to 37.5 K, where the old two-point curve gave 33.5 K;
  - Mars's CO2 frost point at 610 Pa is 146 to 150 K;
  - a fixture pack adding a made-up "xenoice" with only a curve, density and colour gets ground frost, cloud and a
    condensable listing, with no code change (MP3's check).

## Goldens changed (once, on purpose)
`weather.json`, `weather-swap.json`, `weather-storms.json`, `surface-wear.json`, `surface-cover.json` and the
survey and weather lines of `contract.jsonl`. Reason: below the triple point, saturation now follows the ice's
measured curve instead of Clausius-Clapeyron from the triple point with the liquid's latent heat. Water over ice in
Earth's cold bands moves a little; Earth's eroded volume changes by 0.03 %. Nitrogen, methane and ammonia frost
points move by a few kelvin, and CO and Ar join the condensables.

## Known limits (docs/fudges.md, "Volatile species")
- Below a fit's range the curve is extrapolated.
- Over a liquid, saturation is Clausius-Clapeyron from the triple point.
- Four solid densities are unverified.
- The cloud's latent flux uses one heat per substance.
- The lower ends of the valid ranges are from memory.
- The giants' cloud decks still use their five temperature thresholds. Moving them onto these curves is MP3's last
  step and goes with W11.

# W8 part 2: the exchange between ground ice and air (21 Nov 2026)

## What was built
- **`sse.volatile.exchange`** (`sse_climate::exchange`, settings `climate.exchange`): the one climate's seasonal
  cycle, run with the air's main gas able to freeze out and sublime.
  - **Frost pins the ground.** A band that holds ice, or would cool below the frost point, sits at the frost point.
    The energy it lacks or gains goes into condensing or subliming, at the latent heat from the curve's slope.
    A frosted band radiates its emissivity (0.9) times σT⁴ times the world's greenhouse factor.
  - **The air is what the ground does not hold.** Each step solves for the frost point at which the air's change
    equals the ground's net exchange (regula falsi, Illinois). The vapour curve is tabulated once per cycle.
  - **No ground ice given** (Mars): the air is the inventory, and the given mean pressure holds.
    **Ground ice given** (`Conditions::ground_ice_kg_m2`): the pressure is free, and the ice migrates. A year's
    change is carried forward until a band would run out, and the ground's total is kept.
  - It runs only when the main gas's frost point comes within 2 K of the plain cycle's coldest band, or ice is
    given, and never when the gas is the world's liquid. Earth, Venus, Titan, Jupiter and the Moon are untouched.
- `Climate.exchange` holds, per season: the pressure, the frost point, ice per band and the flux. It also holds the
  swing, the perennial bands, the mean ice and a why line. The climate's band temperatures are the pinned ones,
  so everything downstream reads frosted ground at its frost point.
- **Saltation's lofting** reads the exchange: the share of seasons in which a band holds ice and loses it.
- **Survey** climate summary gains `volatile`: gas, mean, minimum and maximum pressure, swing, the pressure and
  frost point per season, ice per band, perennial bands, and whether it is buffered.

## Tests (`sse-climate::exchange_tests`)
- **Mars** (the review's test):
  - CO2 exchanges, and the swing is 22 to 33 %. It came out at 29 %, against Viking's 25 to 30 %.
  - The given mean pressure holds within 3 %.
  - There are exactly two minima, the deeper in southern winter.
  - Both poles get caps in their winters, and nothing condenses within 40° of the equator.
  - The coldest band sits at the frost point.
- **Pluto**:
  - with 1,000 or 3,000 kg/m² of N2 ice the mean pressure agrees within 20 %, because the ice's temperature sets
    it, not the amount;
  - every season's pressure is the sheet's curve at its frost point;
  - warmer seasons have higher pressure;
  - the ice lofts sand.
- **Bertrand et al. 2024's test**: the same world with only the tilt changed. At Pluto's 122.5°, perennial ice
  lies only within 35° of the equator. At 30°, it lies only poleward of 60°. The inventory is kept within 2 %.
- Earth, Venus, Titan, Jupiter and the Moon exchange nothing.

Cost: Mars about 25 ms, and a buffered Pluto about 90 ms, in release.

## Goldens changed (once, on purpose)
`weather.json` and `weather-swap.json` (the weather fixtures' Mars), and the two `surface_surveyed` lines of
`contract.jsonl`. Reason: the weather Mars's poles now sit at the CO2 frost point and its pressure breathes, and
the survey's climate summary gains `volatile` (null on both surveyed worlds). The surface fixtures' worlds,
`surface-wear.json` and `surface-cover.json`, do not change: none comes within 2 K of its main gas's frost point.

## Known limits (docs/fudges.md, "Moving ices")
- One frost emissivity, and frosted bands use the climate's albedo.
- Only the main gas exchanges.
- Migration is carried forward linearly, with no obliquity cycles and no basins.
- Pluto's ice takes the climate's mean temperature (40 K in the fixture, so about 12 Pa against the real 1 Pa).
- Surveyed bodies have no ground ice yet. The surface's Body carries none, so Pluto and Triton surveys run
  unbuffered until S17 or formation gives inventories.
- No polar hood cloud, no CO2 snow and no condensation flow. These are W8 part 3, with S17.
