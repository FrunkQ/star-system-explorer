# SG1 to SG3: stars that glow, churn and flare

Alex, 24 Nov 2026 16:41: yes to "Star glow and flares: stars that glow past their edge, a surface that visibly churns, and flares and mass ejections you can see, with bloated giants looking lumpy". Earlier (23 Nov): "a star in True that looks dynamic, with flares that are real".

## What it does

- **Glow past the edge (SG1).** Nothing glows round a star in space; the halo in every photograph is light scattered in the eye or lens. The glow is the CIE general disability glare equation (Vos and van den Berg 1999) summed over the drawn disc. True is exposed so a white surface lit by the star reads white, which puts the glare at π · PSF(θ) of white for any star: it outshines a sunlit surface out to about 5° and fades over 20°. It is an angle on the screen, so it is the same few degrees near or far, and it brightens when a flare adds to the star's light. The disc is drawn at its own exposure (the face), so its spots and cells stay visible.
- **A churning surface (SG2).** Each cell lives the kernel's granule life on its own clock (offset per home on the lattice), is born weak, domes up and fades (sin(π · age / life)), and drifts a little; new cells take fresh places, so the surface boils smoothly rather than jumping. A picture standing for many lives averages them (contrast 1/√N), as a long exposure would. The limb is lifted or dropped by the cell under it by the kernel's pressure scale height: on the Sun 0.02% of the radius (round), on a red supergiant a few per cent (visibly lumpy, a few giant cells across).
- **Flares and ejections you can see (SG3).** A flare's white-light patch is the kernel's own (10,000 K over a stated area at the peak); with an ejection it goes off where the ejection leaves (the ejection's direction is fixed in space, so the place on the turning star is that direction plus the star's turn at the peak). On a red dwarf a big flare is a white-hot spot that lifts the glow; on the Sun even an X-class flare is a pixel, as it really is. An ejection's front leaves at its speed inside its cone; its brightness is Thomson scattering, σ_T N (R/r)² / 4 of the disc's, which falls as the fourth power of distance and is about a billion times fainter than the star. It is drawn as a coronagraph shows it (radial filter, log scale) and the note says so.
- **Safety.** No more than three flashes a second: flares are grouped by the third of a screen second their rise begins in, only the largest of each is shown, and each rises over at least a quarter and fades over at least a third of a second. Under reduced motion no flare is shown and the cells hold still; the words give the flare rate.

## Phone

The face and halo are pictures drawn on the CPU and shown as sprites, so WebGL2 phones get the same star as WebGPU. Cost per picture (ten a second): the halo is 160² pixels, the face 16 to 256 pixels across; the glare profile is computed once per disc size (quarter-octave steps).

## SG5a: jets and shed shells, the drawing

`packages/renderer/src/star-shells.ts` draws any nebula descriptor round a star (the shape agreed with "Build light and nebulae", which owns the physics and will publish kinds `jet` and `dust-shell` through read_nebulae). A shell's density is its mass over its volume; a shell thicker than its inner radius is a steady wind falling as 1/r²; lobes pinch a shell to a waist along the axis; a jet is two cones of a steady flow with knots at their dated distances. Each pixel sums density squared (glowing gas) or density (dust) along 160 steps of its line of sight, on a log scale three decades deep, coloured by the descriptor's lines at their wavelengths or, for dust, by the star's reflected light. Tests: `star-shells.test.ts` (3). Screens: `shells-planetary-lobes.png`, `shells-young-jet.png`, `shells-giant-dust.png`. Not yet in the app: SG5b wires it once the kinds are published.
