# A3: Minerva, narrative from what an audience knows

Status: done (19 Nov 2026, agents track); sifter hits wired 21 Nov on the kernel's per-audience `sift`.

## What was built
- **`packages/agents/src/minerva.ts`**:
  - `factsBundle(scene, vocab)`: the facts one audience may know, built only from that audience's K9 scene (through its
    data view), in the player vocabulary's words. Unknown facts are listed as unknown ("Not known to them: Mass (not yet
    scanned)"), never filled in. Events and sifter hits can be added (`StoryEvent`, `StoryHit`), already filtered for the
    audience by whoever supplies them.
  - **The number check** (`numbersIn`, `strayNumbers`): every number in her text, in digits ("1,200", "4.61", "6.5e24")
    or words ("three", "a dozen", "twice"), must be a number in the bundle. Ordinals and "one" are not counted.
  - **The secrecy guard** (`hiddenFrom`, `leaks`): the first guard is structural, since the bundle is the audience's
    scene and nothing else is sent. The second checks her text against what the GM's scene holds and the audience's
    does not (hidden subject names, secret text facts such as GM notes): a hidden name, or two rare words of a hidden
    note, fails the draft. The hidden list is used only on the GM's machine and never sent.
  - `runMinerva`: the same draft, check and fix loop as Orac (`loop.ts`), with `checkNarrative` as the check. A draft
    that fails goes back with the stray numbers named (the secret is never repeated in the feedback) and is never shown.
    What passes is `{label: "narrative", audience, text}`.
  - V3's AI context fields carry over as the GM's notes: what about (seedText), voice (style), length.
- **Panel** (`MinervaTab.svelte`, Agents, Minerva tab): pick the audience (from the seat plan), what about, voice and
  length; "The facts she will be given" shows exactly what is sent; Write; the narrative is labelled "Narrative · for
  <audience> · every number checked against their facts". A failed draft shows only why it was not shown.
- Minerva and Orac are offered only on the GM seat (the players' page does not load the agents at all).

## Honest limits
- **Sifter hits** (21 Nov): the shell asks the kernel's `sift` with `audience` (the stack's sifters plus the story
  finder's starters), which sifts only what that audience's scene shows and skips event patterns. `hitsFor` takes the
  best five and uses a result only when the kernel says it sifted for exactly that audience, so an older kernel that
  ignored the field (and returned the GM's hits) gives none. Proved in `minerva.test.ts`: the GM's sift finds the
  secret, the table's finds only the rumour, and the GM's result is refused for the table.
- Chronicle events are not passed: events are not yet filtered per audience, so passing them could leak. Event-pattern
  sifters are skipped for an audience for the same reason.
- The number check is strict: a narrative may not say "three moons" unless the facts hold 3. Unit conversions she does
  herself ("about 100 km" from "98 km") are refused, by design.
- The leak guard is a second line and works on words; it would miss a paraphrase with no shared rare word. The first
  line (never sending what the audience has not been shown) is what makes the guarantee.
- Narrative is shown to the GM to copy or read out. Sending it to players' screens (as a record note with a disclosure,
  J1) comes with the story finder (U8).
- Tested with scripted mock providers. No model has been measured for the Describing job yet.
