# RW17 part 1: measured motion, and eclipses on time

Track RW (real catalogues), design `handover/design/SSE4-real-catalogues-design.md` section 6A.2. Thread "Make eclipses happen on time", 26 Nov 2026.

## What it does

A catalogued body may name a **motion model** (`orbit.motion`, a table id) beside its orbit. Inside the model's span `orbits_at` puts the body where the model says; outside it the body follows a plain orbit fitted to the model at the span's edge. Every placed time says which: `at[].motion` is `measured` or `plausible`, and `Placed.motion` gives the model and its span in universe seconds (or why the model cannot be used). A system reads its models' dates through `time.j2000_s` on its root star, the universe second of J2000.0 TT. Generated systems name no model and are unchanged.

base-sol now names models for the eight planets (VSOP87A; Earth's table is the Earth-Moon barycentre, which is how the Jacobi tree places Earth's group) and the Moon (ELP/MPP02, the DE405 fit), and carries `time.j2000_s = 63,115,200,000` on the Sun, the same anchor the Sol today pack's calendar uses.

## Where the tables came from

The cloud build machine cannot reach IMCCE or CDS. The numbers were taken, as data only (no downloaded code was run), from two open-source packages on the package registries:

- VSOP87A: the `vsop87` crate 3.0.0 (MIT or Apache-2.0), a transcription of IMCCE's VSOP87A files. The cut tables match IMCCE's own check values (vsop87.chk, as the crate's tests copy them) within the cut's stated bound.
- ELP/MPP02 (DE405 fit): `astronomia` 4.3.0 `data/elpMppDeFull.js` (MIT), the published series with each term's arguments combined into one polynomial. Its terms of use at source are unverified (docs/citations/real-catalogues.md M2, PR 638). The Python port that M2 warns about was not used.

Both are cited to their authors (Bretagnon and Francou 1988; Chapront and Francou 2003) in each table and in base-sol. `scripts/check-sol-motion.py`, run on AC-ProArt, checks the copies against JPL Horizons; `build-sol-motion.py` also reads IMCCE's own VSOP87A files if they are fetched there.

## Traps found (read before touching shadows)

- **The Moon's light time is worth 37 seconds of eclipse.** A shadow at Earth now was cast by the Moon about 1.3 s ago. In the system's frame the Moon moves about 30 km/s with Earth, so that is some 40 km, or about 20 arcseconds, or about 37 s of eclipse time. With positions all read at the same instant, every solar eclipse is 36 s late; with the Moon read 1.3 s earlier, each lands on the published second. For a lunar eclipse it is Earth that is read earlier. The Sun's 8 minutes do not matter: it moves metres a second in this frame. **EC1's drawing (PR 617) reads all bodies at the same instant, so its shadows are about 37 s late.** That is inside a minute, but the fix is one line in the shadow placement: offset the caster by its light time.
- **Earth's group is placed by its barycentre.** A model for a body with moons must give the group's barycentre (VSOP87A's EMB), not the body's centre.
- **Universe seconds are TT.** The series run on TDB; the two differ by under 2 ms and are treated as one.
- **The base app's Gregorian calendar puts universe second 0 at 1 January 2000**, while base-sol and Sol today put J2000.0 at 63,115,200,000. Without the Sol today pack's calendar a date shown in the base app is not the date the Moon is drawn at. Not changed here; it belongs to whoever owns the campaign calendar.
