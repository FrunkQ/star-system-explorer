## Hall thruster: SPT family (first run in space 1 March 1972); SPT-100 figures

**Brophy 1992.** Brophy, J. R. 1992, Stationary plasma thruster evaluation in Russia, JPL Publication 92-4 (NASA-CR-192823), https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930016017/downloads/19930016017.pdf

- **Flight history (p. 4):** "On March 1, 1972, the SPT was first operated in space." Also: "Only the SPT-50 and SPT-70 models have been flown while the SPT-100 model... has been flight qualified in the USSR."
- **Claimed SPT-100 figures (Table 1, p. 2):**
  - specific impulse 1600 s
  - thrust 0.080 N
  - input power 1.35 kW
  - efficiency 0.50
  - thruster mass 4 kg
- **Nominal point (Appendix B, p. 24):** 1640 s, 0.084 N and 1344 W.

**Sankovic 1993.** Sankovic, J. M., Hamley, J. A. and Haag, T. W. 1993, Performance evaluation of the Russian SPT-100 thruster at NASA LeRC, IEPC-93-094 (NASA TM-106401), https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19940019157/downloads/19940019157.pdf

- Measured at the design point of 300 V and 4.5 A: "1610 s and 0.50" (test segment 3).
- The other segments gave 1550 s with 0.47, and 1590 s with 0.49.

**Not yet sourced:** the SPT-100's own later flights, on Western geostationary satellites from the 1990s, are not in these texts.

## Size floor: critical mass

Gwin, R. and Mee, W. T. 1959, Critical assemblies of uranium metal, Y-1248, Union Carbide Nuclear Company, Oak Ridge Y-12 Plant, https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4246875

- Bare sphere of uranium metal enriched to about 93 to 94 % U-235, density about 18.5 g/cm3: experimental critical mass 53.9 kg U (Table I, p. 5).
- Water-reflected sphere: 24.9 kg U (Table II and Figure 2, p. 6). The OCR is unclear about which column this value belongs to.

Plutonium (Jezebel) was not found.
