# S3: wearing down (19 Nov 2026)

Surface design section 2.3; research report section 3.2 stage 3. Needs S1, S2 (done).
Physics track, overnight, at the coordinator's request.

## What was built
- `crates/sse-surface/src/wear.rs`, settings under `provides.other.surface.wear` in `packs/base-surface`
  (version `surface-wear-v1`). Runs on the S2 plate grid (24,576 cells).
- `WaterHistory{wet_from_gyr, wet_until_gyr, oceans, pressure_bar}` says when the world had surface water.
  `WaterHistory::steady` is the default reading of a body (wet for its life with today's water).
- `base_heights` gives the starting surface: the S2 plate heights for a plate world, the S0 sketch otherwise.
- `wear(..)` steps in epochs of 100 Myr (a plate world wears only its last 100 Myr window, since S2 heights
  already carry worn-down belts):
  - Routing: priority-flood fill (Barnes et al. 2014), then steepest descent on the filled surface. With no
    sea the lowest cell is the one closed basin's floor.
  - Rain: `rain_m_yr` × a floor, scaled by `sqrt(oceans)`, smoothed over `rain_cells`.
  - Incision: stream power E = K Q^m S solved implicitly in drainage order (Braun and Willett 2013):
    `f = K·Q^m·dt/L`, `h = (h + f·h_r)/(1 + f)`. Glaciated cells (cold by lapse rate) erode `glacial_factor`
    times harder.
  - Sediment is routed downstream, filling pits and then the sea. The volume balance closes exactly.
  - Hillslopes: conservative diffusion over a symmetric edge list (an effective coarse-grid value).
  - Sea level is refitted each wet epoch and frozen after the last one.
- Output `Wear`: heights, receivers, area, discharge, stream length, river, lake and pit flags, incision,
  sediment, glaciated and dune flags, eroded and deposited volumes, per-epoch records.
  - `pit`: in a filled basin (filled minus height over 2 × `fill_epsilon_m`), wet or dry. `lake` is a wet pit.
  - Dunes: dry (rain under `dune_rain`), air of at least `dune_pressure_bar`, slope under `dune_slope`.
- `wear_world(..)` runs the whole chain for a body. `Atlas::from_worn(..)` is the worn atlas: heights from the
  worn grid plus S1's detail octaves, and a per-sample `flow` layer (river, lake, glacier, dune bits).
  - The tile hash includes `flow` only when non-empty, so the S1 and S2 goldens did not change.
  - "flow and channels (S3)" leaves the tile's `pending` list when flow is present.

## Checks (`cargo test -p sse-surface wear`)
- `sediment_balance_closes`: eroded equals deposited plus what reached the sea, to rounding.
- `drainage_is_dendritic_and_follows_hacks_law`: more than 10 confluences; Hack exponent 0.45 to 0.8.
- `no_river_runs_uphill_in_a_thousand_worlds`: on the true surface, every receiver is downhill except inside
  filled basins (lakes or dry pits).
- `an_old_dried_world_keeps_softened_valleys`: a Mars-like world wet to 1 Gyr. At 4.5 Gyr its valleys are less
  steep than at 1 Gyr (mean height step to neighbours falls), and at least half are still cut over 50 m.
- `the_worn_atlas_has_channels`: river bits in level-0 tiles.
- `golden_wear`: `tests/golden/surface-wear.json`, new in this package (no existing golden changed).
- Harness: `surface-wear` added to the wasm engine list; Node harness passes all 27 engines. The Chromium run
  was not repeated this time (the local web server was not allowed in this session); the wasm is the same.

## Numbers seen
- Earth fixture over its 100 Myr window: 6.6e17 m³ eroded, Hack exponent 0.673, 322 river cells, 264 lake
  cells, deepest incision 2.8 km.
- Mars wet to 1 Gyr: 45 epochs, 3.6e18 m³ eroded, deepest incision 7.9 km.

## Known limits
- Cells are about 150 km on Earth, so rivers are trunk systems only; fine valleys are not carved into tiles
  yet (tiles show the cell-level flow layer).
- Diffusion is an effective coarse-grid value, not the local hillslope value (about 0.01 m²/yr).
- Ice and wind are flags plus a glacial erosion factor; no ice-sheet flow or dune migration.
- Sea level is fixed after the last wet epoch; no later loss of water by escape is modelled here.
