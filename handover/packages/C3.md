# C3: reverse placement in a system

Status: done (18 Nov 2026), kernel track, branch `claude/project-thread-dzkz4p`. Classification design section 7, and section 12 row C3. It needs C1, K8 and L0.

## What was built
New crate `sse-place`, with settings in a new pack `packs/base-place` (`provides.other.placement`).
- **Forward model** (`model.rs`): causes go through the real base-physics flows to the facts the atlas classifies.
  - Causes are what can be set: orbit, eccentricity, mass, make-up, albedo, volatile inventory as a pressure, main gas, O2 and CO2 partial pressures, liquid inventory and kind, and an interior ocean.
  - Three flows run as they are: `sse.base.star-main-sequence` (luminosity at the system's age), `sse.base.planet-climate` (equilibrium and grey-greenhouse surface temperature) and `sse.base.planet-bulk` (density, gravity, escape velocity).
  - Radius comes from the same relations as F6 and the atoms: Zeng for rock, a water term for ice, Lopez and Fortney for envelopes, and Chen and Kipping for giants.
  - No flow publishes these links yet, so they are pack settings, each with its source and labelled rough:
    - greenhouse optical depth from pressure, one power law through Earth and Venus;
    - air kept or lost (the Zahnle and Catling cosmic shoreline);
    - where water and methane are liquid (Clausius-Clapeyron);
    - surface dose from the air column.
- **Placement** (`Placer::place`), the four steps of section 7:
  1. **Quick bounds**, each recorded as a plain line:
     - the class's temperatures become an orbit band around this star (grey greenhouse over the class's pressure range, albedo prior);
     - the requested scope;
     - stable orbits (10 mutual Hill radii from every body already there);
     - for classes that need air, the orbits where a body of the class's mass keeps it.
     - Empty bounds give the blocking reason ("the class needs an orbit at 0.32 to 12 au, outside the requested 30 to 40 au").
  2. **Search**:
     - 48 candidates are drawn with keyed draws (`place.search.v1`, from seed and address) inside the bounds, narrowed by the class's typical values.
     - Each runs forward and is scored with the recognition fit, and the recogniser must give the class back.
     - The best 4 are refined over 24 fixed keyed steps.
     - The same request always gives the same answer.
  3. **Cross-references**: the `needs` of every class the result would carry, in every scheme, are settled by the suite that owns them.
     - A `life:<capability>` need is asked of the life suite over the body's habitat graph (L0's `HabitatGraph` and `query_feasible`).
     - An `authored:<what>` need can only be met by an authored intervention.
     - C2's atlas gained four needs: nitrogen-oxygen air needs oxygenic photosynthesis, living needs life, inhabited needs an authored settlement, and terraformed needs authored terraforming.
  4. **One of four outcomes**:
     - **Placed**: full fit, recognised, needs met.
     - **Closest here**: the reasons, including each failing axis with its value and the change needed.
     - **Needs a helping hand**: the interventions. With no life model installed, an oxygen world is "seed oxygenic life", because the life suite says it cannot tell.
     - **Forced**: the class's core values are written over every result that misses, marked authored-impossible.
  - Nothing outside the quick bounds counts as placed, however well it scores.
- **`commit`** writes the placement to the chronicle as follows:
  - the causes as a `place.authored` event (Authored);
  - the results as `place.derive` (Derived, citing it);
  - forced values as `place.forced` (AuthoredImpossible);
  - each intervention as `place.intervention` (Authored).
- **`Placer::preview`**: the picker. Every base class of a scheme is shown as available, available with help, or not possible here, with the reason.
- **Fixtures**: `tests/fixtures/place/systems.json` has four stars:
  - the Sun at 4.6 Gyr, with Venus, Mars and Jupiter in place;
  - a 0.75 solar mass K dwarf;
  - a 0.3 solar mass M dwarf with a hot Neptune;
  - a young 1.3 solar mass F star.

## Checks (`cargo test -p sse-place`)
- **Round trip**: nine requests across the four stars are each placed (or need help) at full fit, then written to a chronicle, read back from its state and classified again as the requested class. They are:
  - temperate ocean (Sun);
  - cold desert and water-rich (K dwarf);
  - airless and pressure-suit (M dwarf);
  - hot greenhouse and carbon-dioxide air (F star);
  - an ammonia-cloud gas giant (Sun, at about 19 au);
  - shirt-sleeve (Sun, needs help).
- **Impossible requests return the blocking reason**:
  - A temperate ocean at 30 to 40 au says the orbit is outside the scope, and that the closest result there is a cold desert at 64 K.
  - A cold desert at 5 to 5.4 au is "too close to Jupiter to be stable".
  - A hot greenhouse at 0.11 to 0.16 au around the F star "loses its air: the star strips it".
- **Forced placements carry the mark**: the far temperate ocean forced writes its surface temperature, liquid and cover as authored-impossible in one marked event, and then reads back as a temperate ocean. An inhabited world cannot be placed by physics. Forced, it records the authored settlement it needs.
- **Picker**: around the Sun the temperate ocean and molten surfaces are available. At 30 to 40 au the temperate ocean is not possible, with the reason, and frozen is available. Every unavailable class has a reason.
- **Identical everywhere**: the same request gives the same placement, and another seed gives other causes. The golden `tests/golden/place.json` (ten placements with their content hashes) is identical natively, in Node and in Chromium (the `place` engine scenario).

## Known limits
- The rough links (greenhouse, shoreline, dose, liquids) are stand-ins until surface and atmosphere flows publish those facts. Each is one pack setting to replace.
- Stability is a spacing rule against existing bodies, not an integration.
- A placement is a single body; moons and belts come later.
- Classes whose results no forward link yet produces can only be forced. Examples are a magnetic field (unshielded) and rings. Inhabited and terraformed are authored by nature.
- Galaxy scale (C5) reuses the quick bounds against patch envelopes.
