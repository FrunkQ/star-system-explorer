# W10a and K-c: Jupiter's chromophore, Titan's haze depth, particle sizes

Asked 24 Nov 2026 by "Build giant bands, clouds and hazes" (W10a) and by the visual polish design section 12 (K-c, Alex yes 24 Nov 18:57). Owner: "Build shared climate and weather". Shipped in PR 418 with K-a and K-b.

## What the weather cells now carry

- `Aerosol.particle_radius_um` and `Layer.particle_radius_um`: a typical radius in µm. The key is left out where no measured size is held, so cells without one read as before.
- Giants whose top deck is ammonia ice (Jupiter and Saturn) gain a `chromophore` aerosol in every cell:
  - optical depth at 1 µm goes from 0.06 over zones to 0.6 over belts, drawn by the zone weight;
  - effective radius goes from 0.15 µm (zones) to 0.29 µm (belts).
  - Colour is a stand-in, [0.55, 0.27, 0.13] linear sRGB, until the data sheet's chromophore row (MP33) lands. Then the colour should read the sheet.
- The ammonia ice deck's radius is 1.0 µm.
- Titan's organic haze optical depth goes from 3 (a stand-in) to 9.6 at 500 nm, with a radius of 0.72 µm (the aggregates' equal-volume radius).
- Dust storms carry 1.5 µm.

## What was read

- **Sromovsky et al. 2017** (arXiv 1706.02779).
  - Abstract, verbatim: colour varies "by varying particle size from 0.12 μm to 0.29 μm and 1-μm optical depth from 0.06 to 0.76".
  - The layer runs from the main cloud top up to 0.9 of its pressure (section IV.4).
  - Per region at medium phase (tables 6 to 8):
    - Equatorial Zone 0.05 to 0.06;
    - South Equatorial Belt 0.49 to 0.76;
    - Great Red Spot about 0.2;
    - North Equatorial Belt 0.16 to 0.55.
  - The table values were read through a summary of the page.
- **Braude et al. 2020** (arXiv 1912.00918): tropospheric cloud radius 1.0 µm and chromophore radius 0.05 µm. The chromophore is no deeper than 0.2 bar in the Great Red Spot and 0.7 bar elsewhere. We keep Sromovsky's radii for the chromophore.
- **Huygens DISR results slides** (Tomasko et al.). Optical depth power laws:
  - above 80 km: 1.012e7 λ^-2.339;
  - 30 to 80 km: 2.029e4 λ^-1.409;
  - below 30 km: 6.270e2 λ^-0.9706.
  - At λ = 500 nm these give 4.9, 3.2 and 1.5, which sum to 9.6. The slide does not state the unit; nm is the only reading that gives sensible numbers.
  - The aggregates are 3000 monomers of 0.05 µm, with an equal-volume radius of 0.72 µm and a projected-area radius of 2.03 µm.
  - **Open:** check against the 2008 paper itself (Planet. Space Sci. 56, 669).
- **Lavvas, Yelle and Griffith 2010**, section 3.1: monomers of 54 nm, about 4000 of them at 100 km.

## Open

- Saturn uses Jupiter's chromophore numbers. Baines et al. 2019 has not been read.
- Water, methane and sulphuric acid clouds carry no size yet (Earth's water clouds about 10 µm; Venus mode 2 about 1 µm, Hansen and Hovenier 1974). Add them when their papers are read.
- Plume and ring particle sizes belong to the plumes and rings threads. They use the same key.
