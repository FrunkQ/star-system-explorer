# O12 (kernel side): the spectrum as it reaches an observer

Design: `SSE4-nebulae-and-light-design.md` section 5B (the spectrum chart); stage O12 in section 8. Module
`sse_light::observe`; kernel op `read_spectrum`. The chart itself is the shell thread's (U track).

## What was built
- `observe(target, observer, table) -> Spectrum`. A target is one or more components (a double star, a star and
  its planet, a nebula), each a `LightDescription` with a line-of-sight speed and, for a glowing photosphere, its
  temperature. Each component is Doppler-shifted exactly (O7) and thinned by 4πd², then summed.
- `Spectrum`:
  - `samples` `[λ, flux density W m⁻² m⁻¹, covered 0/1]` on the pack grid;
  - flux per band;
  - `lines`: emission lines (flux at the observer) and absorption lines (strength 0 to 1) at their observed
    wavelengths, per component, each marked covered or not;
  - `readings` with Why: flux in the observer's bands, temperature from the peak (Wien, from a 400-point scan
    plus golden-section refinement, only if a covered range holds the peak), line-of-sight speed per component
    from its first covered line, doubled lines (resolved when the split exceeds 1/R).
- Observer: `coverage` (named wavelength ranges; none means the GM's full view) and `resolving_power` (default 5000).
- Pack `base-light` gains six Fraunhofer and molecular lines (Ca II K and H, CH G band, Mg b, Fe E, TiO 705.4 nm)
  and an `absorption` table: line strength against temperature (3200 to 40 000 K), after the MK classification
  criteria (Gray and Corbally 2009). `LightTable.absorption` is optional, so other packs need not give one.
- Kernel op `read_spectrum { targets: [{ address, name?, distance_m, components: [{ conditions | light,
  radial_velocity_m_s, photosphere_k? }] }], observer? }` → `spectrum_read { spectra }`; error `bad_spectrum`.
  A component given as conditions shows absorption lines when it shines by its own power at 2000 K or more.

## Evidence (`cargo test -p sse-light`, 5 new tests in `mod o12`)
- The Sun from 10 pc: temperature from the peak is 5772 K to 0.1 %; Ca II K and H, the G band, Mg b, Fe E, Na D,
  Hα and Hβ are listed; TiO and He II are not; flux equals σT⁴·4πR²/4πd² to 0.1 %.
- A 20 000 K star shows He I and no Na or TiO; a 3200 K star shows TiO and Na D and no helium.
- A double star at ±30 km/s: doubled lines at R = 10⁴ (speeds read back to 10 m/s), blurred into one at R = 2000.
- An optical-only observer reads the temperature and a 12 km/s speed and has the infrared greyed; a radio-only
  observer gets no temperature, no speed, all lines uncovered, and a Why saying the peak is outside its bands.
- An Hα emission line arrives with power/4πd² and blueshifted when approaching.

## Stand-ins (to add to docs/fudges.md)
- Absorption strengths are qualitative (0 to 1) and the same for every metal content; O4's line-strength grid
  replaces them. No line widths yet (spin and turbulence broadening), no dust reddening between.
- Photosphere threshold for absorption lines when given as conditions: shines by its own power at 2000 K or more.

## Not done
- Dust and gas between (extinction and interstellar lines): needs the galaxy's dust (G track) and O4.
- Line widths, changing speeds over days (the wobble is in O13), and infrared excess from belts and discs.
- Light travel time (the observer sees the target as it was): with O14 and the campaign switch.
