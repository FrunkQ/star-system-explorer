# F10c: the star's end on the Formation screen

Asked by Alex in the thread "Build the Formation screen", 26 Nov 2026: "once a star bloats and dies, that should be shown here and the state of the solar system afterwards, as they will fight ... nothing will likely happen much after that." Design: `design/SSE4-system-formation-design.md` section 2, stage 9's close ("the star swells, sheds mass, orbits widen, inner worlds are swallowed, and the remnant cools"). Parts 1 and 2: `F10a.md`, `F10b.md`.

## What it adds
- **Ageing the survivors to the star's end**, model in `ui-contract/src/formation-fate.ts`:
  - `fatePlan` builds the time model's SystemSpec from the run's own record. The star is the run's primary (mass, metals). The worlds are the lasting tracks, each with its mass, its orbit now and its eccentricity after the upheaval.
  - The look ahead reaches 3% past the star's `life.t_end`, so the remnant is seen cooling. It runs through the existing kernel op `look_ahead`, on a branch that is discarded at once, so the state hash never moves.
  - `chartFate` reads the answer: when hydrogen runs out, the giant begins and the envelope is shed; the star's radius over time; each world's widening orbit, or when it was swallowed; and the time model's own reasons, cited.
  - `withFate` runs the chart on to the end. Lasting tracks follow the widened orbits, a swallowed world's track stops with a cross where the surface reached it, its event joins the chart, and stage 9 runs to the new foot.
- **A star that outlives the chart is said, not run.** A star whose life passes 100 billion years (`FATE_LIMIT_MYR`, about seven times the universe's age), which means every red dwarf, gets one line: it burns for so many trillion years, and nothing ends within the chart. The time model's giant fits are for stars that do end. A red dwarf below about 0.25 Sun never swells into a giant, yet the fits would draw one (see "Found on the way").
- **On the screen** (`FormationScreen.svelte`, `FormationChart.svelte`, new `FateStrip.svelte`):
  - The main chart shades everything after now as "one plausible future", with a dotted "now" rule. A band marks the star's swelling and death, and swallowed tracks end in a cross.
  - The close-up, "The star's end", sits below the panels. Distance runs across on the main chart's scale. Down the page is the time left before the star sheds its shell, on a log scale, so its last few million years, when it swells fastest and swallows its worlds, get room. The star is the shaded swell from the left, and each world's track widens or stops at the surface. A strip at the foot shows the remnant and what is left round it. Then comes the ending in words, and what it rests on.
  - A "☉ The star's end" button follows the nine stages. Past now, the slice is the screen's own: it says it is looking ahead, and the campaign's date and the time ruler stay where they were.
  - The story card adds each world's ending line, either "swallowed by the swelling star at 0.22 AU" (in the accent) or "the star is a white dwarf; its orbit has widened from 2.5 to 4.3 AU".
  - While the time model works (about 5 s for a Sun-like star) the screen says "Working out how the star ends…".

## Not changed
- No kernel change and no golden change. The time model (sse-time, T2) is used as it is.
- Desks as F10a: Explore and Build. The look ahead is a GM op, as the screen is.

## Found on the way, for the time track
- The time model ages a 0.26 Sun red dwarf through a red giant that reaches past 1 AU, at 0.6 trillion years. Real stars below about 0.25 Sun never become giants (Laughlin, Bodenheimer and Adams 1997). This screen does not show that case: it stops at `FATE_LIMIT_MYR`. The giant tables are already noted as a sketch.
- A look ahead to 650 Gyr took about 38 s in Node, against about 5 s to 13 Gyr: the step count follows the span.

## Checks
- `packages/ui-contract/test/formation-chart.test.ts`, a new test, "the star's end". A red dwarf (u:kestrel/sys:3) outlives the chart and says so. A 0.74 Sun star (u:kestrel/sys:5) has every lasting world aged. Looking writes nothing. Hydrogen runs out after now and before the end. The remnant is a white dwarf and the giant reaches past 0.5 AU. Some worlds are swallowed and some are left, and the swallowed ones are the nearest, each where the surface was. Each survivor's orbit widens by about the star's mass ratio. The chart runs to the end, tracks run down the page, and the stories carry the ending lines.
- `tests/ui/formation.e2e.mjs`: the close-up appears for first light (a 1 Sun binary), with the swell, the band on the main chart and the ending in words, or else the outlives line. "The star's end" moves the slice past now, and the state hash holds.
