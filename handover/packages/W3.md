# W3: giants: storm families and polar regimes (19 Nov 2026)

Track W (weather design `design/SSE4-weather-design.md` sections 3, 6 and 8). New module `crates/sse-weather/src/giants.rs`
(tests in `giants_tests.rs`), new pack key `provides.other.weather_giants` in `packs/base-weather/pack.json` (the existing
`weather` key is unchanged), new fixtures `tests/fixtures/weather/giants.json`, new golden `tests/golden/weather-giants.json`,
example `cargo run -p sse-weather --example giants`. `lib.rs` gains only `pub mod giants;`. Nothing in `rules.rs` changed:
the wiring into `RuleBlock` is left to W2's author (see Integration).

## What was built
- `GiantSettings` (`weather_giants`): every threshold and rate, each with its source; 11 references, all with DOIs.
- `layout(gs, c) -> GiantLayout`, from the conditions only:
  - **Bands**: the S6 cloud deck's layout (`sse-surface` `regimes::cloud_deck`): mass = g a² / G; ice giant below 50 Earth
    masses; U = 150 m/s (gas) or 400 m/s (ice); jets = round(sqrt(a Ω / U)); 2 × jets bands with edges evenly spaced in
    sin(latitude). The edges are identical to S6's for the same inputs (tested against S6's formula and the surface pack's
    numbers). Zones and belts are mirrored about the equator: equatorial zone, then belt, zone, ... so zones carry
    anticyclonic shear and the prograde jets sit on their poleward edges (Vasavada and Showman 2005).
  - **Winds** `zonal_wind`: −cos(π x) per band (x = |sin φ| × jets), retrograde jets half the prograde (S6's ratio);
    equatorial superrotation on gas giants (0.6 U), one broad retrograde equatorial jet on ice giants (−U at the equator,
    prograde poleward of 30°, peak near 60°).
  - **Circumpolar jet**: one polar Rhines length from the pole, L = (U a² / 2Ω)^⅓ (Rhines arrest on the polar γ-plane).
    Saturn: 78.6° (hexagon about 78°N). Its speed is 0.8 U.
  - **Deformation radius and Burger number**: L_d = N H / f, f = 2Ω sin φ, H the 1 bar scale height, one N for every giant
    fitted so Saturn's polar Bu = (L_d / a)² is 1.6 × 10⁻³. Bu decides the polar regime per pole:
    diffuse cap if internal heat < 0.1 W/m² (O'Neill et al. 2015: weak forcing, no polar cyclone) or Bu ≥ 4 × 10⁻³;
    cyclone ring if Bu ≤ 6.25 × 10⁻⁴ (a / L_d ≥ 40); single polar vortex if Bu ≥ 1.11 × 10⁻³ (a / L_d ≤ 30); between,
    drawn per pole (so the poles can differ). Ring counts are drawn per pole from 5 to 9 (Juno: 8 north, 5 south).
  - **Polygon jet** (rule fitted to Saturn): a pole whose regime is `single-vortex` carries a polygon when its circumpolar
    jet is at or above 70°, runs at 100 m/s or more, and its latitude circle holds 3 to 8 waves of k deformation radii:
    waves = 2π a cos φ_jet / (k L_d(φ_jet)), sides = clamp(round(waves), 3, 8), k = 5.07 fitted so Saturn gives 6.00.
    Fewer waves: the jet is too broad for its circle to meander as a polygon; more: it breaks into eddies.
- `giant_systems(gs, c, date) -> Vec<WeatherSystem>`: the systems alive on the date. Keyed draws
  `Draws::new(Seed::from_text(&c.address.to_string()), c.address, Stream)` with streams `weather.giants.storms.v1` and
  `weather.giants.poles.v1`; no other input, so the same on every device.
  - Polar (permanent, `born` = `giants::ALWAYS` = f64::MIN, `ends` = f64::MAX): `cyclone-ring` (count = ring size) plus a
    central `cyclone` and the ring's `cyclone`s at the latitude where they pack (diameter 4 L_d, gap 0.5: Jupiter north
    83.6°, Juno about 83°), or `polar-vortex`, or `diffuse-cap` (to the circumpolar jet), plus `polygon-jet` (count = sides).
  - Storm families, each drawn per era window keyed by the window's index (ids `oval:{window}:{j}`,
    `string:{window}:{j}:{member}`, `dark-spot:{window}:{j}`, `outbreak:{orbit}`), so a storm has the same id, birth, end,
    latitude and 12-point life track from whichever date it is asked for; its longitude on the date is
    lon₀ + u(φ) (day − born) / (a cos φ), u the local zonal wind:
    great ovals (gas giants, anticyclones in zones poleward of the equatorial one, 0.02 a year, life log-uniform 10 to 300
    years, half to nine tenths of the zone's width), strings of 4 to 9 small ovals along a zone (gas, 0.15 a year, 0.5 to
    8 years), dark spots with bright poleward companions (ice giants, 15 to 45°, 0.2 a year, 1 to 6 years, 4000 to 13000
    km), planet-circling outbreaks (gas giants at or below 150 K at 1 bar, one chance of 0.85 per orbit, 200 days,
    wrapping the planet in 55 days: Saturn's Great White Spots, Sánchez-Lavega et al. 2011).
  - Every `why` starts "Rule" and names its condition and source; evidence `rule` (regime and polygon fixed by the
    conditions) or `drawn` (storms, ring counts, transition poles).
- `prepare(gs, c, date) -> GiantPrepared` and `giant_cell(gs, c, &prepared, lat, lon) -> Cell`: three cloud decks
  (condensates by temperature after Sudarsky et al. 2000: Jupiter water / ammonium hydrosulphide / ammonia ice, ice giants
  ammonium hydrosulphide / hydrogen sulphide ice / methane ice), high cloud thick in zones and thin in belts; winds from the jets in every layer, the circumpolar jet
  following the polygon's edge where there is one (the polygon's inside has thinner high cloud); ovals brighten and spin
  anticyclonically, dark spots clear the high cloud with a bright companion poleward, cyclones and the polar vortex spin
  cyclonically (vortex with a clear eye), diffuse caps add haze, outbreaks add a cumulonimbus band that spreads round the
  planet, with virga. `giant_tile(gs, c, date, key, n)` gives a whole `FieldTile` (same cell grid as `RuleBlock::field`).

## Results (`cargo run -p sse-weather --example giants`)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Saturn | 89 | 8 | 2329 km | 1.6e-3 (fitted) | 25 | 78.6°, waves 6.00 | single vortex / single vortex | hexagon at both poles | ovals; Great White Spot outbreaks (1 to 5 in four orbits) |

- **Jupiter → ring**, both poles (a / L_d 68 ≥ 40). Ring counts drawn: 8 north (Juno: 8), 6 south (Juno: 5; a sixth
  joined later, from memory). Ring cyclones 4100 km across at 83.6°N.
- **Saturn → single vortex with a hexagon**: a / L_d 25 ≤ 30; jet at 78.6° (observed about 78°N), 6.00 waves.
- **Uranus → diffuse cap**: both reasons apply, internal heat 0.04 W/m² (O'Neill et al.: too weak a forcing for a polar
  cyclone) and Bu 6.0e-3 ≥ 4e-3 (ice-giant-like, Brueshaber and Sayanagi's 1e-2 regime). Matches its bright, speckled
  polar caps in visible light.
- **Neptune → single vortex, no polygon**: internal heat 0.43 W/m² is enough forcing and Bu 3.3e-3 sits just under the
  diffuse threshold, so its poles get one vortex (Neptune does show a warm south polar region); its circumpolar jet is at
  66°, below 70°, so no polygon. It is the giant nearest a boundary: a 20 % larger L_d would make it a diffuse cap.
- **200 random giants** (keyed draws: radius 3 to 15 Earth radii, mass 8 to 1500 Earth masses, spin 5 to 40 h, 50 to
  1200 K, internal heat 0.01 to 20 W/m², molar mass 2.2 to 2.7 g/mol): 29 of 400 poles (7 %) carry a polygon, with all side
  counts 3 to 8 seen and each equal to the stated relation recomputed from the conditions. Regimes: 112 ring, 46 single,
  242 diffuse cap (low internal heat is common in that draw).

## Known limits
- **S6 inconsistency found**: `sse-surface` `CloudDeck::at` makes the band just south of the equator a belt (its comment
  says both bands either side are zones) and its band winds alternate across the equator rather than mirroring. W3 keeps
  S6's band edges exactly but mirrors zones, belts and winds about the equator, so the S6 globe and the W3 field disagree
  on which southern bands are belts. Fix S6 (one `abs`) or accept.
- The parent's sketch asked for "the most poleward prograde jet from the band layout, if above 70°": with edges evenly
  spaced in sin(latitude) the last edge below the pole is at asin((J−1)/J), above 70° only with 17 or more jets, so no
  real giant would qualify. W3 places the circumpolar jet by the polar Rhines length instead (see fudges).
- The rule cannot tell Saturn's poles apart: both get a hexagon (real Saturn has one only in the north). Poles differ only
  through ring counts and transition draws.
- One wind scale per class (150 or 400 m/s) means Saturn's equator is 90 m/s (real about 400) and the jet-speed condition
  is not yet discriminating.
- Storms keep their latitude (no equatorward drift of dark spots, no mergers like Oval BA's); great ovals do not interact.
- No lightning or precipitation numbers on giants (virga only in outbreaks), no temperature offsets.
- Mass comes from g a² / G (Jupiter 304 M⊕ against 318 real, from rounded fact-sheet values).
- The polar cell geometry uses an azimuthal-equidistant approximation near the pole.

## Integration (wired 19 Nov: `RuleBlock` has a `giants` field; field, cell, systems and report use the giant path for the `banded-giant` regime; the kernel `weather` op serves Jupiter; contract golden gains one line)
- Settings: `let gs = sse_weather::giants::GiantSettings::from_packs(stack)?` (or `giants::base_settings()` for the base
  pack); keep it beside `WeatherSettings` in `RuleBlock` (e.g. a `giants: GiantSettings` field).
- `RuleBlock::field` when `circulation(...).regime == "banded-giant"` (or `!c.solid_surface`):
  `let gp = giants::prepare(&gs, c, date);` once per tile, then for each `(lat, lon)` from `cell_dirs`/`lat_lon`:
  `giants::giant_cell(&gs, c, &gp, lat, lon)`. `giants::giant_tile(&gs, c, date, key, n)` does exactly this and can be
  returned directly.
- `RuleBlock::systems` for giants: `giants::giant_systems(&gs, c, date)` then append the pins (and drop drawn systems
  whose ids a pin already holds).
- `climate` for giants is untouched (W1's path); `giants::layout(&gs, c)` gives jets, Bu, regimes and `why` lines for
  explain mode.
