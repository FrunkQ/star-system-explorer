# R1: pass library, render recipes, degrade order, governor, quality badge

Status: done (19 Nov 2026), renderer track, branch `claude/project-thread-l1e9pw`, PR into `main`.
Authority: `design/SSE4-surfaces-and-rendering.md` sections 5 and 6 (accepted 18 Nov); `design/SSE4-surface-design.md` section 9 (R1 row); handoff 8.9.

## What was built (three lines)
1. A pass library (code) and render recipes (data, `sse.render-recipe/1`) with a checker, knobs, a degrade order, cost estimates and family stand-ins (`packages/renderer/src/recipe.ts`, `passes.ts`, `src/recipes/*.json`).
2. A frame-time governor with hysteresis and back-off, and a quality badge that says what was reduced and why (`governor.ts`).
3. The Body globe now runs on recipes: GPU (three.js WebGPURenderer, WebGL2 fallback, one TSL shader), 2D canvas, or words, all from the kernel's S1 tiles and the same shading numbers (`surface.ts`, `canvas.ts`, `gpu-globe.ts`, `runtime.ts`, `views/body`).

## Design sections implemented
- 5.1 pass library and recipes: four passes (`relief-globe`, `graticule`, `contours`, `text-narration`), each with inputs, tiers, a cost model and an Orac-first `ParamSpec` schema (the V11 shape). Recipes name passes and set parameters; **human knobs** (Relief, Detail) move several deep parameters at once.
- 5.1 sharing rule (decision 7): `contains_code` recipes are rejected; nothing in this release runs recipe code.
- 5.2 degrade order and fall-back: steps are `render_scale`, `set`, `drop` or `tier` (falls to a plainer tier), each optionally per tier and each with the words the badge shows. A recipe without the tier in use hands over to one of its `family` that has it, and the badge says so.
- 6.1 tiers: `gpu-basic`, `canvas` and `text` run. `gpu-high` and `still` are R2 and R3.
- 6.2 probe: minimal. It asks whether WebGPU or WebGL2 starts at all and picks the richest tier the recipe declares that the device can draw. The user can override it from the badge ("Draw on").
- 6.3 governor: exponential smoothing of frame time; down after 10 frames over budget; up after 90 frames under 60% of budget and only if the richer level is predicted to fit (estimate × this device's measured ratio); a step up that has to be taken back doubles the wait. Badge: "Full quality", else "Reduced: <last step>", with every step, the measured frame time against the budget, and what would lift it.
- Shading: hill shading after Horn (1981), from the elevation layer's real slopes with an exaggeration for the shading only; liquid cover is flat. Canvas and GPU shade from the same per-sample normals and colours, in display (sRGB) values, so they match. Linear HDR lighting is R2's.
- Rendering rules: the globe is unit-sized at the origin with the camera three radii away, so the GPU sees only small numbers. Descent and true-scale views (R2) must use `frames.ts`.

No deviation from the design needed an ADR.

## Known limits and what was not done
- **Not a real phone.** The governor check runs on Chromium's emulation. Headless WebGL is SwiftShader (CPU); a real phone GPU behaves differently. The governor works from measured frames, so it adapts either way, but the numbers above are not a phone's.
- **GPU frame time** is measured as the interval between frames while spinning; the draw call's own time (about 1 ms) is only CPU submission. Timestamp queries are not used yet.
- **Probe** only checks that a GPU path starts; it does not benchmark passes or read adapter limits (the design's fuller probe comes with `gpu-high` in R2).
- **Level 0 tiles only** (about 25 km a sample on Earth). Face-edge slopes are one-sided, so faint seams can show in strong relief.
- **Recipes are not editable in the app yet.** They are documents in the repo; the Styles screen does not list them. Orac editing is R4.
- Horn (1981) DOI `10.1109/PROC.1981.11918` was written from memory; verify.

## Plan for the rest of the track (as agreed with the coordinator, 19 Nov)
- **R2** Body and descent views on `gpu-basic` and `gpu-high`: linear HDR lighting from the star's light, Hillaire atmosphere ported to TSL, a 2D cloud layer that reads the simple weather track's cloud ops when they exist (stand-in clouds marked as such until then), finer tiles by what is in view, and the line-terrain, contour, wireframe and shaded descent recipes. **Magnetic field overlay** joins R2 as a pass (`field-lines`): dipole field lines and magnetopause from each body's dipole moment, tilt and offset, which the physics thread's B1 package will publish; until then a marked stand-in from mass and rotation is not drawn (no invented physics), and V3's own overlay is not copied.
- **R3** `still` tier and "take photograph" to Media.
- **R4** Orac edits recipes with preview frames.
- **After R4 (R5, proposed): exotic objects through the light lenses.** Accretion discs, jets, coronae and beams drawn from the emission-by-band the physics thread's B1 publishes, viewed per band (visible, X-ray, gamma and the rest) through the O1 light switch, including pulsar and magnetar fields. The pass library's `inputs` already name scene fields, so these plug in as new passes and recipes.
